# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Chest Pain

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 65-year-old male presenting with acute onset, crushing central chest pain radiating to the left arm and jaw. Started 2 hours ago while gardening. Associated with sweating and nausea.

## Findings
- HR: 98 bpm, BP: 145/90 mmHg, RR: 22, SpO2: 96% on room air.
- Patient appears distressed, diaphoretic.
- Cardiovascular exam: normal heart sounds, no added sounds or murmurs.
- Chest clear to auscultation.
- ECG: ST elevation in leads II, III, aVF (Inferior STEMI).

## Assessment
Acute Inferior ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI). High risk presentation.

## Learning Points
- Immediate recognition of STEMI criteria on ECG.
- Activation of primary PCI pathway.
- Use of antiplatelets (e.g., Aspirin, Ticagrelor) and antithrombotics per local protocol.
- Consideration of right ventricular involvement in inferior STEMI.
