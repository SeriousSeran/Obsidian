# Deidentified Clinical Case - CAP

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 68-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a 3-day history of worsening shortness of breath, a productive cough with rust-coloured sputum, and right-sided pleuritic chest pain. He reports feeling generally unwell, with subjective fevers and rigors.

Past medical history: Hypertension, Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, Ex-smoker (quit 5 years ago, 30 pack-year history).
Social history: Lives independently at home.

## Findings
Observations:
- Temperature: 38.9°C
- Heart Rate: 110 bpm
- Respiratory Rate: 26 breaths/min
- Blood Pressure: 105/65 mmHg
- SpO2: 91% on room air

Examination:
- Alert and oriented (AMTS 10/10).
- Tachypnoeic and visibly using accessory muscles of respiration.
- Right lower zone: Dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, and coarse inspiratory crackles. Increased vocal resonance in the right lower zone.

## Assessment
The patient is presenting with a classic picture of right lower lobe lobar pneumonia (Community Acquired Pneumonia).

Initial investigations should include:
- CXR (to confirm consolidation)
- FBC, U&Es, CRP, LFTs
- Blood cultures and Sputum culture
- ABG (given hypoxia)

CURB-65 calculation:
- C: No (AMTS 10/10) = 0
- U: Pending, but assuming normal for now = 0
- R: Yes (RR 26 < 30? Wait, RR is 26, so < 30. Let's re-read criteria. RR >= 30. So 0). Wait, 26 is high, but not >= 30.
- B: No (BP 105/65) = 0

- Current score: 1 (Low severity, but needs clinical judgement given hypoxia).

*Correction on CURB-65 calculation in practice:* Despite a low score, the patient is hypoxic and tachycardic, warranting admission for oxygen therapy and IV antibiotics initially or close observation with oral antibiotics.

## Learning Points
- **Classic Presentation:** Rust-coloured sputum, high fever, and rigors are classic for *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.
- **Clinical Signs of Consolidation:** The triad of dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, and increased vocal resonance strongly points to alveolar filling (consolidation).
- **CURB-65 Caveats:** The CURB-65 score is a tool, not a rule. A score of 1 typically suggests outpatient management, but this patient's hypoxia (SpO2 91%) and general malaise may necessitate admission.
