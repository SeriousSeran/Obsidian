# Deidentified Clinical Case

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 68-year-old male presents with a 3-day history of worsening shortness of breath, a productive cough with rust-colored sputum, and subjective fever. He reports right-sided chest pain that worsens on inspiration. Medical history includes hypertension. He is a former smoker (quit 10 years ago).

## Findings
- **Vitals:** Temp 38.5°C, HR 110 bpm, RR 26/min, BP 100/60 mmHg, SpO2 91% on room air.
- **Respiratory Exam:** Reduced chest expansion on the right. Dullness to percussion at the right base. Auscultation reveals bronchial breathing and coarse crackles over the right lower zone.
- **Investigations:** Chest X-ray showed right lower lobe consolidation. FBC showed elevated WCC (15 x 10^9/L). CRP was 120 mg/L.

## Assessment
Community Acquired Pneumonia (CAP). CURB-65 score is 2 (Urea pending, but RR > 30 and Age > 65), indicating moderate severity, warranting hospital admission for monitoring and IV/oral antibiotics depending on clinical judgment and local guidelines.

## Learning Points
- Typical presentation of CAP includes fever, productive cough, and pleuritic chest pain.
- Classical respiratory signs: focal dullness, crackles, and bronchial breathing.
- Importance of CURB-65 for risk stratification.
