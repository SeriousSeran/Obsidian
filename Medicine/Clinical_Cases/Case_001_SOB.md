# Deidentified Clinical Case: SOB

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
- 68-year-old male presenting with a 3-day history of progressively worsening shortness of breath, productive cough with rusty sputum, and fever. No chest pain. Background of mild hypertension, non-smoker.

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 105, BP 110/70, RR 24, Temp 38.5C, SpO2 93% on room air.
- **Examination:** Looks flushed and unwell. Reduced chest expansion on the right side. Dullness to percussion over the right lower zone. Auscultation reveals bronchial breathing and coarse crackles over the right lower base.

## Assessment
- Likely community-acquired pneumonia (CAP).
- **CURB-65 Score:** 1 (Age > 65), mortality risk is low/intermediate, suitable for home treatment or short-stay hospital assessment depending on clinical judgment and social factors.

## Learning Points
- Typical signs of consolidation include bronchial breathing and dullness to percussion.
- Importance of calculating CURB-65 for any suspected pneumonia to guide management setting (home vs hospital).
- Typical organism to consider is Streptococcus pneumoniae.

## Follow Up
- Patient required initial IV antibiotics and oxygen, stepped down to oral amoxicillin after 24 hours. Advised on follow-up chest X-ray in 6 weeks to ensure resolution.
