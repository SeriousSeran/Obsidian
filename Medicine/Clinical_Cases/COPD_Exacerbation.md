# Deidentified Clinical Case - COPD Exacerbation

## Presentation
68-year-old male presents to the Emergency Department with increasing shortness of breath and productive cough (yellow/green sputum) over the past 3 days. He reports feeling more breathless on minimal exertion than his usual baseline. Known history of COPD.

## Findings
- **Obs:** RR 24, SpO2 88% on room air, HR 110, BP 130/80, Temp 37.8°C.
- **Chest Exam:** Barrel chest, use of accessory muscles. Reduced chest expansion. Hyper-resonant to percussion. Widespread expiratory wheeze and coarse crepitations on auscultation.

## Assessment
Acute exacerbation of COPD, likely infective in origin given the change in sputum colour and low-grade fever. Type 1/Type 2 respiratory failure needs to be assessed via ABG.

## Learning Points
- **NICE Guidelines:** Target SpO2 for known COPD retainers is 88-92% until ABG results are available to rule out Type 2 Respiratory Failure.
- **Management:** Oxygen (titrated), nebulized bronchodilators (Salbutamol/Ipratropium), systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone), and antibiotics if purulent sputum or signs of infection.

## Follow Up
Patient responded well to initial nebulizers and oxygen therapy. Admitted for observation and continued treatment. ABG showed mild Type 2 respiratory failure which improved with controlled oxygen.
