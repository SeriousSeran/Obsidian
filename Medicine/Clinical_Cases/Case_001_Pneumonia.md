# Case 001 - Pneumonia

## Presentation
A 68-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a 3-day history of worsening shortness of breath, a productive cough with yellow-green sputum, and right-sided chest pain that worsens on deep inspiration. He reports feeling feverish and having chills.

Past medical history: Hypertension, type 2 diabetes mellitus, ex-smoker (20 pack-years, quit 10 years ago).

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 110 bpm, BP 105/65 mmHg, RR 26/min, SpO2 92% on room air, Temp 38.6°C.
- **Examination:** The patient appears unwell and tachypneic. Chest auscultation reveals decreased breath sounds, coarse crackles, and dullness to percussion over the right lower lung zone.
- **Investigations:**
  - CXR: Patchy consolidation in the right lower lobe.
  - Bloods: WCC 18.5 x 10^9/L (neutrophilia), CRP 150 mg/L, Urea 8.2 mmol/L, Creatinine 105 umol/L.

## Assessment
The patient has Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) involving the right lower lobe.
CURB-65 Score: 2 (Age > 65, Urea > 7). This suggests moderate severity, warranting consideration for hospital admission.

## Learning Points
- Typical presentation of lobar pneumonia.
- Importance of calculating the CURB-65 score to guide management location (outpatient vs. inpatient) and antibiotic choice.
- Distinguishing pleuritic chest pain from other causes of chest pain.

## Follow Up
Patient admitted for IV antibiotics (e.g., Co-amoxiclav and Clarithromycin) and supplemental oxygen. Plan to step down to oral antibiotics once afebrile and clinically improving for 24 hours. Repeat CXR in 6 weeks to ensure resolution of consolidation and rule out underlying malignancy, especially given his smoking history.
