# Deidentified Clinical Case: Respiratory

## Presentation
A 65-year-old retired builder presented to the Emergency Department with a 3-day history of worsening shortness of breath, a productive cough with rust-colored sputum, and fevers. He reported sharp, right-sided chest pain that was worse on inspiration.

**Background:** Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus, 40-pack-year smoking history (quit 5 years ago).

## Findings
- **Vitals:** Temp 38.5°C, HR 110 bpm, RR 26, SpO2 91% on room air, BP 105/65 mmHg.
- **Respiratory Exam:** Reduced chest expansion on the right side. Dullness to percussion at the right base. Coarse crackles and bronchial breathing heard over the right lower zone.

## Assessment
The clinical presentation is highly suggestive of a right lower lobe pneumonia. The patient is tachycardic, tachypneic, hypoxic, and febrile, indicating a significant systemic response.

## Learning Points
- **CURB-65 Score:** Consider using the CURB-65 score to assess pneumonia severity and guide management (Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30, Blood pressure <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic, Age ≥65).
- **Pleuritic Chest Pain:** The sharp pain worse on inspiration is classic pleuritic chest pain, commonly seen in pneumonia when the infection involves the pleura.
- **Bronchial Breathing:** Indicates lung consolidation (fluid/exudate replacing air).

## Follow Up
Patient admitted for IV antibiotics, supplemental oxygen, and fluids. CXR confirmed right lower lobe consolidation.
