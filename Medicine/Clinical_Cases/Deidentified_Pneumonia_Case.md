# Deidentified Clinical Case: Community Acquired Pneumonia

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 68-year-old male presents to the emergency department with a 3-day history of worsening shortness of breath, fever, and a cough producing rust-colored sputum. He reports feeling generally unwell and has experienced rigors at home.

**Past Medical History:**
- Hypertension
- Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

**Social History:**
- Ex-smoker (quit 10 years ago, 30 pack-year history)
- Lives with his wife, independent with ADLs.

## Findings
**Observations:**
- HR: 110 bpm
- RR: 26 breaths/min
- SpO2: 92% on room air
- Temp: 38.6°C
- BP: 105/65 mmHg

**Examination:**
- Alert and oriented (Abbreviated Mental Test Score 10/10).
- Chest examination reveals reduced chest expansion on the right side.
- Dullness to percussion over the right lower zone.
- Coarse crepitations and bronchial breathing heard over the right lower zone on auscultation.
- Increased vocal resonance on the right side.

## Assessment
**Primary Diagnosis:**
Community-Acquired Pneumonia (Right Lower Lobe).

**Severity Assessment (CURB-65):**
- Confusion: No (0)
- Urea: Pending, assuming normal for now (0)
- Respiratory rate: >30? No (0) - *Wait, RR is 26, which is <30*
- Blood pressure: <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic? No (0)
- Age >= 65: Yes (1)
- Current Score: 1 (Pending Urea results). If Urea > 7, score becomes 2.

**Initial Plan:**
- Supplemental oxygen to maintain SpO2 94-98%.
- Bloods: FBC, U&Es, CRP, Blood cultures.
- Sputum culture.
- Chest X-ray (PA).
- Commence empirical antibiotics as per local guidelines (e.g., Oral Amoxicillin if mild, or IV antibiotics if patient deteriorates or Urea > 7).

## Learning Points
- **Classic Presentation:** Rust-colored sputum and rigors are classic textbook signs of Streptococcus pneumoniae infection.
- **CURB-65 Importance:** Crucial for stratifying risk and deciding on inpatient vs. outpatient management. Even with a score of 1 or 2, clinical judgment is key.
- **Signs of Consolidation:** Bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion, and increased vocal resonance are the hallmark signs of lobar consolidation.

## Follow Up
Patient responded well to initial antibiotic therapy. Urea was normal, confirming CURB-65 of 1. Treated as outpatient with oral antibiotics. Scheduled for a repeat chest X-ray in 6 weeks to ensure resolution and exclude underlying lung malignancy.
