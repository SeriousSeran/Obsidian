# Deidentified Clinical Case: Asthma Exacerbation

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
24-year-old female presents to ED with increasing shortness of breath, wheeze, and dry cough over the past 24 hours. She has a known history of asthma since childhood, usually well controlled on low-dose ICS and PRN salbutamol. She recently had a mild viral upper respiratory tract infection.

## Findings
- **Obs**: HR 115 bpm, RR 26, SpO2 93% on room air, Temp 37.4C.
- **Chest**: Widespread expiratory polyphonic wheeze, using accessory muscles, able to speak in short sentences.
- **PEFR**: 45% of predicted best.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation, likely triggered by a recent viral URI.

## Learning Points
- Important to assess the severity of asthma exacerbations (moderate, severe, life-threatening) as it dictates the urgency and type of management.
- Initial management for severe asthma includes high-flow oxygen, nebulized SABA (salbutamol) and ipratropium bromide, and systemic corticosteroids.
- Always check inhaler technique and compliance before discharge.
