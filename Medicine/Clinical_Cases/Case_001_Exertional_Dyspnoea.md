# Deidentified Clinical Case: Exertional Dyspnoea

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A middle-aged patient presented with a 3-month history of progressively worsening shortness of breath on exertion, now limiting their ability to climb a single flight of stairs. No chest pain, palpitations, or orthopnea.

## Findings
On examination, the patient was comfortable at rest. Heart sounds were dual with no murmurs. Chest auscultation revealed fine inspiratory bibasal crepitations. No signs of right heart failure.

## Assessment
The differential diagnosis included interstitial lung disease (ILD), early heart failure (though less likely given no orthopnea/murmur and fine rather than coarse crackles), and chronic pulmonary infection. Initial workup planned: High-resolution CT (HRCT) chest and pulmonary function tests (PFTs).

## Learning Points
- Fine inspiratory bibasal crackles are a classic sign of interstitial lung disease (like pulmonary fibrosis).
- Differentiating heart failure (coarse crackles, often with orthopnea and elevated JVP) from ILD based on history and examination is key.
