# Deidentified Clinical Case: Asthma Exacerbation

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 24-year-old individual presented to the emergency department with acute onset of severe breathlessness and wheezing that began 4 hours ago. They reported a history of asthma but had not been using their preventer inhaler regularly. They had used their reliever inhaler 10 times prior to presentation with minimal relief.

## Findings
On examination, the patient was visibly distressed, speaking in partial sentences.
- Respiratory rate: 28 breaths/min
- Heart rate: 115 bpm
- SpO2: 92% on room air
- Chest auscultation revealed widespread bilateral polyphonic expiratory wheeze.
- No silent chest, no paradoxical pulse.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation, likely triggered by viral upper respiratory tract infection or allergen exposure, compounded by poor preventer adherence.

## Learning Points
- Recognizing the criteria for "severe" asthma (HR > 110, RR > 25, inability to complete sentences in one breath, PEF 33-50% of best).
- The importance of immediate administration of nebulized bronchodilators and systemic corticosteroids.
- The critical need to check SpO2 and initiate oxygen therapy.

## Follow Up
The patient was treated with high-flow oxygen, continuous salbutamol nebulizers, and oral prednisolone. Symptoms improved significantly over 2 hours. They were observed, provided with an updated asthma action plan, and referred back to their primary care provider for regular preventer optimization.
