# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Severe Asthma

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 22-year-old female presents to the Emergency Department with severe shortness of breath, wheezing, and a non-productive cough that started 3 hours ago. She has a known history of childhood asthma but has not used an inhaler in several years. She reports a recent upper respiratory tract infection. She is unable to complete sentences in one breath.

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 115 bpm, RR 28/min, SpO2 93% on room air, Temp 37.2°C, BP 110/70 mmHg.
- **Respiratory Exam:** Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze bilaterally. Use of accessory muscles of respiration (sternocleidomastoid).
- **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF):** 45% of her predicted best.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation, likely triggered by a viral upper respiratory tract infection. Key indicators of severity include inability to complete sentences, tachycardia (>110), tachypnea (>25), and PEF < 50%.

## Learning Points
- **Severity Assessment:** It is critical to differentiate between moderate, severe, and life-threatening asthma. Severe features include PEF 33-50%, RR >25, HR >110, or inability to complete sentences.
- **Initial Management:** Immediate administration of high-flow oxygen, nebulized salbutamol (often with ipratropium), and systemic corticosteroids.
- **Monitoring:** Continuous SpO2 monitoring, repeat PEF after bronchodilator therapy, and close observation for signs of exhaustion or respiratory failure.

## Follow Up
Patient responded well to initial nebulizers and steroids. Symptoms improved, and PEF increased to >75% of predicted. Discharged after 4 hours of observation with a short course of oral prednisolone, a newly prescribed ICS/LABA preventer, a SABA reliever, and an updated asthma action plan. Scheduled for primary care review in 48 hours.
