# Case 001 Acute Asthma

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 22-year-old university student presents to the Emergency Department with progressive shortness of breath over the past 2 days. Reports a history of childhood asthma, previously well-controlled with a PRN salbutamol inhaler. Over the last 48 hours, has been needing the inhaler every 2 hours with minimal relief. Reports a recent upper respiratory tract infection. Cannot complete full sentences without pausing for breath.

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 115 bpm, RR 26/min, SpO2 93% on room air, BP 125/80 mmHg, Temp 37.4°C.
- **Respiratory:** Widespread bilateral polyphonic expiratory wheeze. Accessory muscle use visible (sternocleidomastoid). Good air entry bilaterally, no silent chest.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardic, normal heart sounds, no murmurs.
- **PEFR (Peak Expiratory Flow Rate):** 45% of predicted for height/age.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation, likely precipitated by a viral upper respiratory tract infection.
Severity criteria met: Tachycardia >110, Tachypnea >25, PEFR 33-50% best/predicted, inability to complete sentences.

## Learning Points
1. **Severity Classification:** Crucial to quickly classify asthma exacerbations (Moderate vs. Severe vs. Life-Threatening) to guide immediate therapy.
2. **Silent Chest:** Always auscultate carefully; absence of wheeze in a highly distressed patient might indicate a 'silent chest' (very poor air entry), a sign of life-threatening asthma.
3. **Steroids:** Early administration of systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone) is vital in acute exacerbations to reduce airway inflammation.
