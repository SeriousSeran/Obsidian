# Deidentified Clinical Case

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 22-year-old female presents to the Emergency Department with severe shortness of breath and wheezing that has progressively worsened over the last 12 hours. She has a known history of asthma but ran out of her preventer inhaler two weeks ago. She has been taking multiple puffs of her salbutamol inhaler with no relief. She is visibly distressed and speaking in short phrases.

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 125 bpm, RR 32 breaths/min, SpO2 91% on room air, Temperature 37.1°C.
- **Examination:** Sitting upright, using accessory muscles of respiration. Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze audible on auscultation. No silent chest. PEF is 40% of her predicted value.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation, likely triggered by medication non-compliance (lack of inhaled corticosteroids) or a mild viral upper respiratory infection.

## Learning Points
- Recognition of acute severe asthma: Inability to complete sentences in one breath, RR > 25, HR > 110, or PEF 33-50% predicted.
- Immediate management priorities are restoring oxygenation and relieving bronchospasm.
- The absence of wheeze ("silent chest") in a severe asthma attack is a life-threatening sign, indicating minimal air movement.

- Prescribed a short course of oral prednisolone.
- Re-issued inhaled corticosteroid preventer and educated on inhaler technique.
- Asthma action plan updated and scheduled for review in the primary care clinic within 48 hours.
