# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Dyspnea

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
- 24-year-old male presenting to ED with acute worsening of shortness of breath over the last 6 hours.
- Complains of dry cough and chest tightness.
- History of childhood eczema and allergic rhinitis.
- Did not take regular inhalers for the past month.
- Triggered after exposure to cold air during an evening run.

## Findings
- **Vitals:** RR 28, HR 115, SpO2 93% on room air, BP 120/80, Temp 37.0°C.
- **General:** Unable to complete full sentences in one breath. Mild use of accessory muscles.
- **Chest:** Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze on auscultation. Equal air entry bilaterally. No crackles.
- **Other:** No cyanosis, no clubbing. JVP not elevated.

## Assessment
- Acute severe asthma exacerbation.
- Criteria met: RR > 25, HR > 110, inability to complete sentences in one breath.
- Does not currently meet life-threatening criteria (SpO2 > 92%, no silent chest, no cyanosis, no exhaustion/confusion).

## Learning Points
- Immediate assessment of asthma severity dictates initial management.
- Salbutamol nebulizers and oxygen titrated to 94-98% are first-line acute interventions.
- Early administration of systemic steroids (e.g., Prednisolone or Hydrocortisone) is crucial to reduce inflammation and prevent relapse.
- Always reassess response to initial therapy closely.
