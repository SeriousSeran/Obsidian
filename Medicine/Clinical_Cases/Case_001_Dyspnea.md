# Clinical Case 001: Dyspnea

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 65-year-old male presents to the ED with acute onset shortness of breath and a productive cough with rust-colored sputum over the last 3 days. He reports subjective fevers and chills.

## Findings
- **Vitals:** Temp 38.5°C, HR 110, RR 24, BP 110/70, SpO2 91% on room air.
- **Respiratory:** Decreased breath sounds and crackles at the right lung base. Dullness to percussion on the right.
- **CXR:** Right lower lobe consolidation.

## Assessment
Community-acquired pneumonia, likely bacterial (Streptococcus pneumoniae).

## Learning Points
- Rust-colored sputum is classically associated with pneumococcal pneumonia.
- A CURB-65 score should be calculated to determine the need for inpatient admission vs outpatient management.
- Early administration of appropriate antibiotics (e.g., amoxicillin or a macrolide depending on local guidelines and severity) is crucial.

## OSCE/Viva Connection
- **OSCE:** Practice respiratory examination, focusing on signs of consolidation (vocal fremitus, dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, crackles).
- **Viva:** Be prepared to discuss the CURB-65 criteria and typical vs atypical pneumonia presentations.

## Follow Up
- Review sputum cultures and sensitivities.
- Monitor clinical response to antibiotics (defervescence, improvement in oxygenation).
- Consider repeat CXR in 6 weeks to ensure resolution and rule out underlying malignancy, especially if a smoker.
