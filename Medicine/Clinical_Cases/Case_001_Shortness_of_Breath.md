# Deidentified Clinical Case: Shortness of Breath

## Presentation
A 24-year-old individual presented to the emergency department with acute onset shortness of breath and right-sided pleuritic chest pain. The symptoms started suddenly while at rest. No history of trauma. Medical history includes a previous episode of similar symptoms 3 years ago. Patient is tall, thin, and a smoker.

## Findings
- **Observations:** HR 110, BP 120/80, RR 24, SpO2 94% on room air.
- **Examination:** Trachea central. Decreased chest expansion on the right side. Hyperresonant percussion note on the right. Reduced breath sounds on the right apex and axilla.
- **Investigations:** Chest X-ray showed a right-sided visible pleural edge with absent lung markings distal to it, estimated at 3cm from the chest wall at the hilum.

## Assessment
Primary spontaneous pneumothorax (right-sided, large).

## Learning Points
- Recognise the classic presentation of primary spontaneous pneumothorax: tall, thin, young male smoker.
- Differentiate between primary (no underlying lung disease) and secondary (underlying lung disease like COPD or asthma) pneumothorax.
- Understand the criteria for intervention (e.g., size > 2cm or symptomatic).

## OSCE/Viva Connection
- **OSCE:** Practice explaining the procedure of needle aspiration or chest drain insertion to a patient. Link to [[Medicine/OSCE/Respiratory_Examination_Station]].
- **Viva:** "What are the indications for a chest drain in a primary spontaneous pneumothorax?" (Answer: failed aspiration, bilateral pneumothorax, haemodynamic instability).

## Follow Up
Patient required a chest drain insertion due to the size. Follow-up chest X-ray showed re-expansion. Discharged with smoking cessation advice and outpatient follow-up.
