# Deidentified Clinical Case 001

## Presentation
- 65-year-old male presenting with 3 days of progressive shortness of breath, productive cough with rust-colored sputum, and fever.
- No history of recent travel. Smoker (20 pack-years).

## Findings
- Observations: T 38.5C, HR 110, BP 100/60, RR 26, SpO2 92% on room air.
- Examination: Decreased chest expansion on the right, dull to percussion right lower zone, coarse crackles and bronchial breathing present right lower zone.

## Assessment
- Working diagnosis: Right lower lobe Community Acquired Pneumonia.
- CURB-65 score: 1 (Age >= 65), low to moderate risk.
- Differentials: COPD exacerbation (less likely given focal signs and fever), pulmonary embolism (focal consolidation on exam favors pneumonia).

## Learning Points
- Typical presentation of pneumococcal pneumonia.
- Importance of CURB-65 score in guiding admission and treatment setting.

- Links to [[Medicine/OSCE/Respiratory_Examination_Station]] (eliciting signs of consolidation).
- Links to [[Medicine/Topics/Community_Acquired_Pneumonia]] for management guidelines.

## Follow Up
- Admitted for initial IV antibiotics due to borderline hypoxia, switched to oral after 48 hours as clinical condition improved. Discharged with plan for 6-week repeat CXR.
