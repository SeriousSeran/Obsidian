# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Asthma Exacerbation

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
24-year-old male presents to the Emergency Department with acute onset shortness of breath and audible wheeze. He reports a 2-day history of a viral upper respiratory tract infection. Known history of asthma since childhood, usually well-controlled on inhaled corticosteroids, but admits to poor compliance recently.

## Findings
- Airway: Patent, but speaking in short sentences.
- Breathing: RR 28/min, SpO2 93% on room air. Widespread bilateral polyphonic wheeze on auscultation. Prolonged expiratory phase. Use of accessory muscles.
- Circulation: HR 115 bpm, BP 125/80 mmHg.
- Disability: Alert and oriented, GCS 15.

## Assessment
Acute moderate-to-severe asthma exacerbation, likely triggered by a viral URTI and exacerbated by medication non-compliance.

## Learning Points
- Recognize the signs of life-threatening asthma (e.g., silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion, poor respiratory effort).
- Initial management prioritizes oxygenation and bronchodilation (Salbutamol nebulizers).
- Importance of systemic corticosteroids in acute exacerbations to reduce inflammation.

Patient stabilized with nebulizers and oral steroids. Discharged with a clear asthma action plan, a short course of oral prednisolone, and an urgent follow-up with their primary care physician.
