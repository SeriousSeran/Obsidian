# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Asthma Exacerbation

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
- 25-year-old female presents to the emergency department with acute onset shortness of breath and wheezing.
- Reports a history of asthma since childhood, usually well-controlled on inhaled corticosteroids and as-needed beta-agonists.
- Symptoms started after exposure to cold air and dust during a house move.
- Complains of chest tightness and difficulty completing sentences in one breath.

## Findings
- **Vitals:** RR 28, HR 115, SpO2 93% on room air.
- **Respiratory:** Diffuse bilateral expiratory wheezes on auscultation. Prolonged expiratory phase.
- **Cardiovascular:** Tachycardia, regular rhythm, no murmurs.
- **General:** Using accessory muscles of respiration. Patient appears anxious and distressed.

## Assessment
- **Diagnosis:** Acute asthma exacerbation (moderate to severe based on RR, HR, and use of accessory muscles).
- **Differentials considered:** Anaphylaxis, panic attack, spontaneous pneumothorax.

## Learning Points
- Recognize the signs of a severe asthma attack (e.g., inability to complete sentences, silent chest, cyanosis).
- Early administration of systemic corticosteroids and inhaled bronchodilators is crucial.
- Monitoring peak expiratory flow (PEF) is an important tool in assessing severity and response to treatment.

- Advised to continue oral prednisone taper and follow up with primary care physician within 3 days.
