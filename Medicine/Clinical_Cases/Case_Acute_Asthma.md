# Deidentified Clinical Case: Acute Severe Asthma

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 22-year-old female presents to the Emergency Department with a 2-day history of progressively worsening shortness of breath and wheezing. She reports waking up multiple times last night due to chest tightness. She has a known history of asthma but has run out of her preventer inhaler. Currently unable to complete sentences in one breath.

## Findings
- **Vitals:** RR 28, HR 115, SpO2 93% on room air, Temp 37.1°C, BP 120/75.
- **Chest Exam:** Bilateral widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze. Prolonged expiratory phase. Use of accessory muscles.
- **Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF):** 45% of her predicted baseline.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation.
- *Severity markers present:* Unable to complete sentences, RR > 25, HR > 110, PEF 33-50% best/predicted.

## Learning Points
- **Severity Classification:** Recognizing the difference between moderate, severe, and life-threatening asthma is critical for acute management.
- **Steroid Importance:** Early administration of systemic steroids (oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone) reduces mortality and relapse.
- **Beta-agonists:** Nebulized or spaced salbutamol is the cornerstone of acute relief.

## Follow Up
Patient was treated with high-dose oxygen, nebulized salbutamol + ipratropium, and oral prednisolone. Symptoms improved after 1 hour. Discharged on a short course of oral steroids and an urgent asthma nurse review, with a strict plan to restart her preventer inhaler.
