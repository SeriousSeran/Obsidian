# Deidentified Clinical Case: Asthma Exacerbation

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Presentation
A 22-year-old university student presents to the Emergency Department with severe shortness of breath, wheezing, and chest tightness that started 2 hours ago. He is unable to complete full sentences. He has a known history of asthma but states he ran out of his preventer inhaler 2 weeks ago.

## Findings
- **Vitals:** RR 30, HR 120, SpO2 93% on room air, BP 130/80.
- **Examination:** The patient is using accessory muscles of respiration. On auscultation, there is a widespread, loud, polyphonic expiratory wheeze. Chest expansion is reduced.
- **Investigations:** PEFR is 40% of his predicted best.

## Assessment
Acute severe asthma exacerbation. The patient is tachycardic, tachypneic, unable to complete sentences, and has a PEFR < 50% predicted, which classifies this as a severe attack.

## Learning Points
- **O SHIT management:** Immediate oxygen, Salbutamol (nebulized), Hydrocortisone (IV) or oral Prednisolone, Ipratropium bromide, and consider Theophylline/Magnesium for life-threatening cases.
- **Signs of severity:** Inability to complete sentences, RR > 25, HR > 110, PEFR 33-50% predicted indicates severe asthma.
- **Silent Chest:** If wheezing stops but the patient is still distressed, it may indicate a 'silent chest' due to minimal airflow, which is a life-threatening sign.

## OSCE/Viva Connection
- **OSCE:** Practice setting up a nebulizer and giving instructions on correct inhaler technique.
- **Viva:** Be ready to list the criteria that differentiate moderate, severe, and life-threatening asthma.

## Follow Up
- Once stabilized and PEFR returns to >75% predicted, the patient can be considered for discharge with a short course of oral corticosteroids.
- Education on inhaler compliance and reviewing his asthma action plan are crucial before discharge.
