# Respiratory Examination Station

> A structured approach to assessing the respiratory system in an OSCE setting.

## Opening
- **WIPER:** Wash hands, Introduce self, Patient details (name/DOB), Explain examination, Request consent.
- Ensure the patient is exposed from the waist up (maintaining dignity) and positioned at 45 degrees.
- Ask if they are in any pain before starting.

## Focused Task
- Perform a comprehensive examination of the respiratory system.

## Findings Sequence
1.  **General Inspection:** Look for respiratory distress, use of accessory muscles, cyanosis, and cachexia. Check surroundings for inhalers, sputum pots, oxygen, or nebulisers.
2.  **Hands:** Check for clubbing (Schamroth's window), cyanosis, tar staining, and fine tremor (beta-agonist use). Asterixis (CO2 retention).
3.  **Pulse and RR:** Assess rate, rhythm, and character. Count respiratory rate while feeling the pulse.
4.  **Face/Neck:** Check for conjunctival pallor, central cyanosis (tongue), elevated JVP (cor pulmonale). Feel for tracheal tug and measure cricosternal distance.
5.  **Chest (Inspection):** Scars, deformities (barrel chest, pectus excavatum/carinatum), asymmetry of movement.
6.  **Chest (Palpation):** Check apex beat. Assess chest expansion (bilaterally, placing hands firmly on chest wall). Tactile vocal fremitus.
7.  **Chest (Percussion):** Percuss supraclavicular, upper, middle, lower zones and axillae. Compare left to right.
8.  **Chest (Auscultation):** Listen to all zones comparing sides. Assess breath sounds (vesicular or bronchial), added sounds (wheeze, crackles, rubs). Assess vocal resonance.
9.  **Back:** Ask patient to sit forward. Repeat inspection, expansion, percussion, and auscultation on the back. Check for sacral oedema.
10. **Legs:** Check for pitting oedema (cor pulmonale) and signs of DVT (erythema, swelling).

## Differentials (Key Pathologies)
- **Asthma:** Expiratory polyphonic wheeze, hyperinflated chest, decreased air entry.
- **COPD:** Barrel chest, tar staining, prolonged expiration, diffuse wheeze, decreased breath sounds.
- **Pneumonia:** Coarse crackles, bronchial breathing, dullness to percussion, increased vocal resonance.
- **Pleural Effusion:** Stony dull percussion, decreased breath sounds, reduced expansion on affected side.
- **Pulmonary Fibrosis:** Fine end-inspiratory crackles (Velcro-like), clubbing.

## Plan
- Mention full set of observations (including SpO2).
- Sputum culture (if productive cough).
- Chest X-ray.
- ABG (if saturations low or suspecting CO2 retention).
- Pulmonary function tests (spirometry).
