# Respiratory Examination Station

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Opening
- Wash hands, introduce self, check patient identity.
- Explain the procedure and obtain consent.
- Ensure the patient is adequately exposed (chest) and positioned at 45 degrees.

## Focused task
- **General Inspection:** Assess work of breathing, use of accessory muscles, cyanosis, inhalers/oxygen at bedside.
- **Hands:** Clubbing, tar staining, peripheral cyanosis, CO2 retention flap (asterixis).
- **Face/Neck:** JVP assessment, central cyanosis.
- **Palpation:** Tracheal deviation, chest expansion, tactile vocal fremitus.
- **Percussion:** Assess resonance over anterior, lateral, and posterior lung fields. Note dullness or hyperresonance.
- **Auscultation:** Listen to all zones for vesicular vs bronchial breath sounds. Note added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rub). Check vocal resonance.

## Findings
- Present positive and pertinent negative findings sequentially.
- E.g., "On examination, the patient had reduced chest expansion on the right side, with dullness to percussion and bronchial breathing in the right lower zone, consistent with consolidation."

## Differentials
- Consolidation (Pneumonia)
- Pleural effusion
- Pneumothorax
- Asthma/COPD exacerbation

## Plan
- Complete full vital signs including SpO2.
- Order an erect Chest X-ray.
- Consider sputum culture, arterial blood gas (ABG) if hypoxic/hypercapnic, and basic blood tests.
