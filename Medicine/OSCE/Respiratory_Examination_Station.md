# Respiratory Examination Station

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Opening
- Wash hands, introduce self (name and role).
- Confirm patient identity and age.
- Explain procedure (examining the chest, which involves looking, feeling, tapping, and listening).
- Gain consent.
- Position patient at 45 degrees, adequately exposed from the waist up.
- Ask about pain.

## Focused task
- **General Inspection:** Inhalers, sputum pots, oxygen, cyanosis, breathlessness, use of accessory muscles, cachexia.
- **Hands:** Clubbing, tar staining, fine tremor (salbutamol), flapping tremor (COPD/CO2 retention), cyanosis.
- **Pulse and RR:** Rate, rhythm, and respiratory rate.
- **Face/Neck:** JVP, conjunctival pallor, central cyanosis (tongue).
- **Chest Inspection:** Scars, shape (barrel chest), symmetry of movement.
- **Palpation:** Tracheal tug/deviation, apex beat, chest expansion (anterior and posterior).
- **Percussion:** Anterior and posterior chest wall, axillae. (Resonant, dull, stony dull).
- **Auscultation:** Breath sounds (vesicular, bronchial), added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rub), vocal resonance. (Anterior, posterior, and axillae).
- **Lymph nodes:** Cervical and supraclavicular (e.g., Virchow's node).
- **Legs:** Peripheral oedema, DVT signs.

## Findings
- Example: "On examination of this patient, there was no use of accessory muscles at rest. Pulse was 80 and regular. Trachea was central and chest expansion was symmetrical. On auscultation, there were bilateral fine end-inspiratory crackles at the lung bases. There was no peripheral oedema."

## Differentials
- Based on findings (e.g., basal crackles):
    - Pulmonary fibrosis
    - Heart failure
    - Bronchiectasis
    - Resolving pneumonia

## Plan
- Thank patient, wash hands, help them dress.
- To complete my examination, I would like to:
    - Take a full history.
    - Obtain a set of full vital signs, including oxygen saturation.
    - Perform a peak flow.
    - Send off sputum for MC&S if applicable.
    - Request a chest X-ray.
