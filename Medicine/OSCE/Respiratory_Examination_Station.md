# Respiratory Examination Station

> OSCE station map for respiratory examination. Not final clinical advice.

## Opening
- Identify patient, wash hands, introduce yourself, explain the examination, and gain consent.
- Ensure patient is positioned at 45 degrees, adequately exposed from the waist up.

## Focused task
- **Inspect:**
  - General appearance (comfortable at rest? breathlessness? cyanosis?).
  - Sputum pots, inhalers, oxygen therapy around the bed.
  - Hands: clubbing, tar staining, peripheral cyanosis, flapping tremor (asterixis).
  - Face: central cyanosis, Horner's syndrome.
  - Neck: JVP, tracheal deviation.
  - Chest: shape (barrel chest, pectus excavatum), scars, symmetry of movement.
- **Palpate:**
  - Tracheal position (if not already done).
  - Chest expansion (apex and bases).
  - Tactile vocal fremitus (optional depending on local OSCE guidelines).
- **Percuss:**
  - Supraclavicular, anterior, lateral, and posterior lung fields. Assess for dullness or hyper-resonance.
- **Auscultate:**
  - Use the bell for supraclavicular areas, diaphragm for the rest.
  - Listen for breath sounds (vesicular vs bronchial), added sounds (wheeze, crackles, rub).
  - Assess vocal resonance.

## Findings
- State present and absent clinical findings plainly.
- E.g., "On examination of this patient, there was evidence of a widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze but no signs of clubbing or central cyanosis."

## Differentials
- Asthma
- COPD
- Pneumonia
- Pleural effusion
- Pulmonary fibrosis

## Plan
- **Bedside:** Check oxygen saturations, respiratory rate, temperature, peak flow (if asthma suspected), sputum sample.
- **Investigations:** Chest X-ray, ABG (if low saturations), blood tests (FBC, CRP).
- Review and escalate if the patient is acutely unwell.
