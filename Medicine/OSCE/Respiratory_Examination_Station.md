# Respiratory Examination Station

> OSCE station map for respiratory examination. Not final clinical advice.

## Opening
- Wash hands, introduce yourself, check patient identity, explain the procedure, and gain consent.
- Position patient at 45 degrees.
- Ensure adequate exposure of the chest.

## Focused task
### General Inspection
- Appearance (comfortable at rest, respiratory distress, use of accessory muscles).
- Sputum pot, oxygen therapy, inhalers nearby.

### Hands & Arms
- Clubbing, cyanosis, tar staining.
- Tremor (CO2 retention flap - asterixis, fine tremor from beta-agonists).
- Pulse (rate, rhythm - bounding in CO2 retention).
- Respiratory rate.

### Face & Neck
- Eyes (Horner's syndrome, pallor).
- Mouth (central cyanosis).
- JVP (elevated in cor pulmonale).

### Chest Inspection
- Scars, deformities (barrel chest, pectus excavatum/carinatum).
- Chest movements (symmetry).

### Chest Palpation
- Tracheal deviation.
- Cricosternal distance.
- Apex beat (displaced).
- Chest expansion (symmetry, normally >5cm).

### Chest Percussion
- Apex to bases, alternating sides.
- Resonant, dull (consolidation, collapse, fluid), stony dull (pleural effusion), hyperresonant (pneumothorax).

### Chest Auscultation
- Breath sounds (vesicular, bronchial).
- Added sounds (crackles, wheeze, rub).
- Vocal resonance.

## Findings
- State present and absent clinical findings plainly to the examiner.

## Differentials
- Asthma
- COPD
- Pneumonia
- Pleural Effusion
- Pulmonary Fibrosis

## Plan
- Complete examination by checking oxygen saturation, temperature, blood pressure.
- Consider PEFR (Peak Expiratory Flow Rate).
- Consider sputum sample.
- Consider Chest X-ray.
