# Respiratory Examination Station

> OSCE station map for respiratory examination. Not final clinical advice.

## Opening
- Identify patient, wash hands, explain, consent.
- Position the patient at 45 degrees, exposing the chest as appropriate.

## Focused task
- **Inspect (End of Bed):** General appearance, respiratory rate, work of breathing, use of accessory muscles, cyanosis, sputum pots, oxygen delivery devices.
- **Inspect (Hands/Face/Neck):** Clubbing, tar staining, cyanosis, flapping tremor (asterixis), pulse, JVP, tracheal position.
- **Palpate:** Chest expansion, apex beat (displacement?), tactile vocal fremitus (or vocal resonance later).
- **Percuss:** Anterior, lateral, and posterior chest wall (compare left to right, assess for dullness or stony dullness).
- **Auscultate:** Breath sounds (vesicular vs bronchial), added sounds (wheeze, crackles, rub), vocal resonance.

## Findings
- State present and absent clinical findings plainly.
- E.g., "The patient has a respiratory rate of 24, bronchial breathing and coarse crackles over the right lower lobe, with dullness to percussion and increased vocal resonance in the same area. There is no clubbing or lymphadenopathy."

## Differentials
- Consider pneumonia (consolidation), pleural effusion (stony dullness), COPD/Asthma (wheeze), pulmonary fibrosis (fine end-inspiratory crackles).
- Explain why each is plausible based on findings (e.g., "The focal signs of consolidation are highly suggestive of a lobar pneumonia").

## Plan
- Investigations: Oxygen saturation, ABG (if hypoxic), CXR, sputum culture, FBC/CRP.
- Mention red flags, escalation, and review.
