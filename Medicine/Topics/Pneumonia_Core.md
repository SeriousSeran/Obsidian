# Pneumonia Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. For study purposes only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs, which may fill with fluid or pus.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Pathogens evade upper respiratory defenses, multiply in the alveoli, and cause an inflammatory response, leading to exudate formation and impaired gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Productive cough
- Fever and chills
- Shortness of breath
- Pleuritic chest pain

### Signs
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Crackles/crepitations on auscultation
- Dullness to percussion
- Bronchial breathing

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Consolidation, infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis and extent |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism | Target antibiotic therapy |
| Blood tests | Elevated CRP/WBC | Indicate systemic infection |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Sputum culture/Gram stain (to identify organism, though often treated empirically first)

**Gold standard investigation ::** Sputum culture

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 score (Confusion, Urea, Respiratory rate, Blood pressure, Age >= 65)

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity (CURB-65)
2. Oxygen therapy if hypoxic
3. Empirical antibiotics based on local guidelines

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccines

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | Varies | First-line for low-severity CAP | GI upset, rash |
| Macrolide/Doxycycline | Varies | Atypical coverage or penicillin allergy | GI upset, QT prolongation (macrolides) |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (often combined with a macrolide for moderate/severe CAP)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Pleural effusion
- Empyema
- Sepsis
- Lung abscess

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis / Septic shock

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and localized crackles on auscultation.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Chest X-ray is needed to confirm the presence of consolidation and rule out other causes of symptoms.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Early administration of appropriate empirical antibiotics and oxygen therapy if hypoxic.
