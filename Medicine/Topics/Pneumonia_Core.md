# Pneumonia

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** Infection of the lung parenchyma characterized by consolidation and exudate in the alveoli.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Microorganisms bypass upper airway defenses, reaching alveoli. This triggers an inflammatory cascade, leading to capillary leak, alveolar exudate, and impaired gas exchange (consolidation).

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Fever, chills, rigors.
- Productive cough (purulent or rust-colored sputum).
- Pleuritic chest pain.
- Dyspnea.

### Signs
- Tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxia.
- Fever.
- Consolidation signs: dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, crackles, increased vocal resonance.

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Focal consolidation, infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis, rule out effusion |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism | Guide antibiotic therapy |
| Blood cultures | Bacteremia in severe cases | Guide antibiotic therapy |
| FBC/CRP | Leukocytosis, elevated CRP | Marker of inflammation/infection |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical findings consistent with pneumonia plus new infiltrates on Chest X-ray.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 (Confusion, Urea > 7, Respiratory rate >= 30, Blood pressure < 90 systolic or <= 60 diastolic, Age >= 65) to assess severity and mortality risk.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity using CURB-65.
2. Oxygen therapy to maintain saturations.
3. Empiric antibiotics based on severity and local guidelines.
4. IV fluids if hypotensive.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccination.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | e.g. 500mg TDS | First-line mild CAP | GI upset, rash, anaphylaxis |
| Doxycycline / Macrolide | Variable | Penicillin allergy / Atypical coverage | GI upset, photosensitivity, QT prolongation (macrolides) |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (for mild Community-Acquired Pneumonia in typical settings).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion.
- Empyema.
- Lung abscess.
- Sepsis / Septic shock.

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis / Septic shock.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and focal crackles/bronchial breathing.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Chest X-ray confirms consolidation and differentiates from other causes of dyspnea like heart failure or pneumothorax.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Assess severity (CURB-65), administer oxygen, and give early appropriate empiric antibiotics.

**What is pneumonia? ::** Infection of the lung parenchyma with alveolar consolidation.

## OSCE Tips

- Remember to check respiratory rate and oxygen saturations early.
- Perform a thorough chest examination, specifically comparing lung zones for dullness and crackles.
- Ask about recent travel, occupation, or contacts to consider atypical organisms.
