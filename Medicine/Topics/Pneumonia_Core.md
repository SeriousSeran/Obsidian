# Pneumonia Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.
> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Pathogens enter the alveoli, multiply, and cause an inflammatory response, leading to fluid or pus filling the air sacs.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Cough (with or without phlegm)
- Fever
- Chills
- Difficulty breathing

### Signs
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Crackles/crepitations on auscultation
- Bronchial breath sounds

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Consolidation, infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis and extent |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism | Target antibiotic therapy |
| Blood culture | Bacteremia | Identify systemic spread |
| FBC | Elevated WCC | Infection marker |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Chest X-ray with clinical symptoms

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 score (Confusion, Urea, Respiratory rate, Blood pressure, Age >= 65)

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen therapy if hypoxic
2. Empirical antibiotics (oral or IV depending on severity)
3. Fluid resuscitation if hypotensive
4. Analgesia/antipyretics

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice
- Vaccination (Pneumococcal, Influenza)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg TDS | Mild CAP | GI upset, rash, anaphylaxis |
| Co-amoxiclav | 1.2g IV TDS | Severe CAP | Diarrhea, hepatic dysfunction |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (for mild CAP)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Pleural effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and shortness of breath.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Chest X-ray is needed to confirm consolidation and differentiate from other causes of dyspnea.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Assess severity using CURB-65 to decide on outpatient vs. inpatient management and appropriate empirical antibiotics.
