# Pneumonia Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the lung parenchyma characterized by consolidation and signs of lower respiratory tract infection.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Pathogens bypass respiratory defense mechanisms (like the mucociliary escalator or alveolar macrophages) leading to inflammation, fluid accumulation, and alveolar consolidation, which impairs gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Cough (often productive with purulent sputum)
- Fever, chills, and rigors
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Pleuritic chest pain

### Signs
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Focal crackles / crepitations on auscultation
- Bronchial breath sounds and dullness to percussion over the affected area

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray (CXR) | Lobar or patchy consolidation | Confirms diagnosis and locates infection |
| FBC (Full Blood Count) | Leukocytosis (high WCC), especially neutrophilia | Indicates bacterial infection |
| CRP (C-reactive protein) | Elevated | Marker of inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray (CXR)

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical signs of lower respiratory tract infection + new consolidation on Chest X-ray.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 score (Confusion, Urea > 7, Respiratory rate >= 30, Blood pressure < 90/60, Age >= 65) to assess severity and need for hospitalization.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity using CURB-65.
2. Provide supplemental oxygen to maintain target SpO2 (94-98% for healthy, 88-92% for COPD).
3. Start empirical antibiotics based on local guidelines (e.g., Amoxicillin for mild CAP, Co-amoxiclav + Clarithromycin for severe).

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg TDS oral | Mild Community Acquired Pneumonia | GI upset, rash, allergy/anaphylaxis |
| Doxycycline | 200mg stat, then 100mg OD | Penicillin-allergic mild CAP | Photosensitivity, esophagitis |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (for mild CAP in non-allergic patients)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion / Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis / Septic shock
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis / Septic shock

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A patient presenting with acute onset fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and localized crackles on auscultation.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** A CXR is essential to confirm the diagnosis of pneumonia by visualizing consolidation, which differentiates it from other lower respiratory tract infections like acute bronchitis.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Immediate assessment of severity (using CURB-65) to determine if inpatient or outpatient management is appropriate, followed by prompt initiation of empirical antibiotics.
