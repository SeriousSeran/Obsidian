# Asthma

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.
> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Airway hyperresponsiveness to stimuli leads to smooth muscle contraction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production, causing airway narrowing.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Use of accessory muscles in severe attacks

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Reversible obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7, >12% increase after bronchodilator) | To confirm airflow obstruction and reversibility |
| Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) | Decreased and variable | Useful for monitoring and diagnosing |
| Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry with reversibility testing

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- British Thoracic Society (BTS) / SIGN guidelines
- GINA guidelines

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (if hypoxic, aim SpO2 94-98%)
2. Salbutamol nebulized
3. Ipratropium bromide nebulized (if severe)
4. Systemic corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone)

### Long-term
- SABA (Short-acting beta2-agonist) as required
- ICS (Inhaled corticosteroid) as preventer
- LABA (Long-acting beta2-agonist) / LTRA (Leukotriene receptor antagonist)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200mcg PRN | Symptom relief (reliever) | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Beclometasone | Varies | Preventer (ICS) | Oral thrush, hoarse voice |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) combined with a SABA for relief.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Life-threatening asthma attack
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Acute severe/life-threatening asthma requiring intubation.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze, cough at night, and a history of atopy (eczema, hay fever).

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is needed to objectively demonstrate the reversible obstructive defect, rather than just relying on peak flow.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack: Assess severity (mild, moderate, severe, life-threatening) based on PEFR, speech, resp rate, and heart rate, then give oxygen and high-dose bronchodilators immediately.
