# Atrial Fibrillation

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending | Not final clinical advice

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A supraventricular tachyarrhythmia characterized by uncoordinated atrial activation and consequently ineffective atrial contraction.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Loss of normal sinus node pacing, replaced by multiple re-entrant wavelets in the atria. Can lead to atrial remodeling and stasis of blood in the left atrial appendage, increasing the risk of thromboembolism.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Palpitations
- Dyspnea
- Fatigue, lightheadedness
- Chest pain (if concurrent ischemia)
- May be asymptomatic

### Signs
- Irregularly irregular pulse
- Variable intensity of the first heart sound (S1)
- Absence of a wave in the JVP

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ECG | Absent P waves, irregularly irregular QRS | To confirm diagnosis |
| Echocardiogram | Structural heart disease, left atrial dilation | To look for underlying causes and assess LV function |
| TFTs | Hyperthyroidism | Important secondary cause |

**Key investigation ::** ECG

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> 12-lead ECG demonstrating typical features.

**Gold standard investigation ::** ECG

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CHA2DS2-VASc score (stroke risk)
- HAS-BLED score (bleeding risk)

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess stability: if hemodynamically unstable, perform urgent synchronized DC cardioversion.
2. Rate control vs Rhythm control based on duration, symptoms, and patient profile.
3. Anticoagulation assessment.

### Long-term
- Rate control (Beta-blockers, CCBs, Digoxin).
- Rhythm control (Antiarrhythmics, cardioversion, catheter ablation).
- Anticoagulation (DOACs or Warfarin) based on CHA2DS2-VASc score.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bisoprolol | 2.5-10 mg OD | Rate control | Bradycardia, hypotension |
| Apixaban | 5mg BD | Stroke prevention (anticoagulation) | Bleeding |

**First-line treatment ::** Beta-blocker (for rate control)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Stroke / Systemic thromboembolism
- Heart failure (tachycardia-induced cardiomyopathy)

**Most serious complication ::** Ischemic stroke

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Irregularly irregular pulse, palpitations, breathlessness.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** ECG to confirm rhythm. Echo to check for structural changes and LV function.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Hemodynamic stability assessment, followed by stroke risk assessment (CHA2DS2-VASc) and rate/rhythm control.

## OSCE Tips

- In a cardiovascular examination, explicitly state the pulse is "irregularly irregular" and check for a pulse deficit (difference between apex rate and radial pulse rate).
- Always mention calculating a CHA2DS2-VASc score when discussing management.
