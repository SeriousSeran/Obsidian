# Heart Failure

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A clinical syndrome characterized by structural or functional impairment of ventricular filling or ejection of blood, leading to inadequate cardiac output.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Initial insult (ischemia, pressure/volume overload) leads to decreased cardiac output, triggering compensatory neurohormonal activation (RAAS, SNS). This causes fluid retention, vasoconstriction, and harmful cardiac remodeling (hypertrophy, dilation), worsening the failure.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea (PND).
- Fatigue, weakness.
- Peripheral edema, abdominal swelling.

### Signs
- Elevated JVP.
- Third heart sound (S3 gallop).
- Bibasilar lung crackles (crepitations).
- Hepatomegaly, pitting peripheral edema.

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| BNP / NT-proBNP | Elevated | Released due to ventricular stretch; high negative predictive value. |
| ECG | Arrhythmias, LVH, prior MI | Identifies underlying etiology or precipitants. |
| Chest X-ray | Cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema, pleural effusion, Kerley B lines | Shows fluid overload and heart size. |
| Echocardiogram | Reduced or preserved LVEF, structural abnormalities | Confirms diagnosis and classifies HFrEF vs HFpEF. |

**Key investigation ::** Echocardiogram.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Echocardiography to assess ejection fraction and structural abnormalities, combined with clinical criteria (e.g., Framingham criteria).

**Gold standard investigation ::** Echocardiogram.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Framingham criteria for diagnosis of congestive heart failure.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Sit patient upright, give oxygen if hypoxic.
2. Intravenous loop diuretics (e.g., Furosemide).
3. Consider nitrates/vasodilators if hypertensive; CPAP/BiPAP if severe respiratory distress.

### Long-term
- Lifestyle: fluid restriction, salt restriction, daily weights.
- Cardiac rehabilitation.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| ACE Inhibitors / ARBs | Titrate to max tolerated | HFrEF, reduces mortality | Hypotension, hyperkalemia, cough (ACEi). |
| Beta-blockers (e.g., Bisoprolol) | Titrate slowly | HFrEF, reduces mortality | Bradycardia, fatigue, bronchospasm. |
| MRAs (e.g., Spironolactone) | Low dose | HFrEF with continued symptoms | Hyperkalemia, gynecomastia (spironolactone). |
| SGLT2 Inhibitors | Standard dose | HFrEF and HFpEF | UTI/genital infections, euglycemic DKA. |

**First-line treatment ::** ACEi/ARB + Beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2i (the four pillars for HFrEF).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Arrhythmias (AF, ventricular arrhythmias).
- Chronic kidney disease (cardiorenal syndrome).
- Liver congestion (cardiac cirrhosis).
- Sudden cardiac death.

**Most serious complication ::** Sudden cardiac death / refractory cardiogenic shock.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Older adult presenting with progressive exertional dyspnea, waking up gasping for air at night (PND), and swollen ankles.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** BNP is excellent for ruling *out* heart failure in an acute breathlessness presentation if the level is low.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute heart failure: decongestion (IV diuretics). In chronic HFrEF: optimizing mortality-reducing medical therapy (the four pillars).

## OSCE Tips

- Ensure you look for peripheral edema, check JVP, and auscultate for basal crackles and an S3.
- Always ask about pillows used to sleep (orthopnea).
