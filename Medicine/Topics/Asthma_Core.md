# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airflow obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, and recurrent episodes of wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, and cough.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Trigger-induced airway inflammation (often eosinophilic/allergic), leading to bronchoconstriction, mucosal edema, and mucus plugging, causing reversible airflow limitation.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic wheeze
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory polyphonic wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles in severe attacks

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7) that is reversible with bronchodilator | Demonstrates reversible airway obstruction |
| PEFR | Diurnal variation > 20% | Shows variable airflow limitation |
| FeNO | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms suggestive of asthma
- Evidence of variable airflow obstruction (Spirometry/PEFR)
- Eosinophilic inflammation (FeNO) in some guidelines

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (if hypoxemic)
2. SABA (e.g., Salbutamol nebulized or via spacer)
3. Systemic Corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone)
4. Ipratropium bromide (if severe)
5. Consider IV Magnesium sulfate or Aminophylline in severe/life-threatening cases

### Long-term
- Trigger avoidance
- Inhaler technique education
- Asthma action plan

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever for symptoms | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| Beclometasone (ICS) | Varies | Preventer for inflammation | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) + short-acting beta2-agonist (SABA) as needed, or increasingly, MART (Maintenance and Reliever Therapy) with ICS/LABA.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Exacerbations / status asthmaticus
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax
- Airway remodeling (fixed airflow obstruction)

**Most serious complication ::** Acute severe/life-threatening asthma leading to respiratory arrest

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze and cough, typically worse at night or after exercise, with a personal or family history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing is chosen because it objectively demonstrates the hallmark of asthma: variable, reversible airflow obstruction.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, the priority is to correct hypoxemia, rapidly relieve bronchospasm with a SABA, and reduce inflammation with systemic steroids.

- Always check inhaler technique in a station involving asthma.
- Provide a clear asthma action plan.
- Look for signs of severe asthma during examination (e.g., unable to complete sentences in one breath, silent chest).
