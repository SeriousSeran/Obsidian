# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Trigger-induced airway inflammation (often IgE mediated) leading to bronchoconstriction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Polyphonic wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles in severe attacks

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7), reversible with bronchodilator | Diagnostic confirmation |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Variability, reduced during exacerbations | Monitoring |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical history supported by objective tests demonstrating variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms suggestive of asthma.
- Documented variable airflow obstruction.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (if hypoxemic)
2. Short-Acting Beta-Agonist (SABA) via spacer or nebulizer (e.g., Salbutamol)
3. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone or Hydrocortisone)
4. Ipratropium bromide (if severe)

### Long-term
- Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Long-Acting Beta-Agonists (LABA)
- Allergen avoidance

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | Variable | Acute bronchospasm | Tachycardia, tremor, hypokalemia |

**First-line treatment ::** ICS-LABA combination inhaler (MART regimen) or ICS + as needed SABA depending on guidelines.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Acute severe asthma (status asthmaticus)
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Life-threatening acute severe asthma requiring intubation.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with episodic wheeze and nocturnal cough, often with atopic history.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry helps differentiate reversible obstruction (asthma) from irreversible (COPD).

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, oxygenation and bronchodilation are immediate priorities.

- Don't forget to mention peak flow measurement in your plan.
