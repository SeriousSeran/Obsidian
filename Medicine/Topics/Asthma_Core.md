# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and airway inflammation.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Triggers cause an immune response (Type I hypersensitivity), releasing inflammatory mediators (like histamine, leukotrienes) that lead to bronchoconstriction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic wheezing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- Use of accessory muscles (in severe attacks)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.70), reversibility with bronchodilator | Confirms diagnosis and reversibility |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Monitors variability and severity |
| FeNO (Fractional exhaled NO) | Elevated (> 40 ppb) | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Demonstration of variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating an obstructive defect with significant reversibility (>12% and >200mL increase in FEV1).

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- History of variable respiratory symptoms AND
- Evidence of variable expiratory airflow limitation.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute (Asthma Exacerbation)
1. Oxygen (titrate to SpO2 94-98%)
2. Salbutamol nebulized or via spacer
3. Ipratropium bromide nebulized (if severe)
4. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone)
5. IV Magnesium sulfate (in acute severe/life-threatening cases)

### Long-term
- Avoidance of known triggers
- Personalized asthma action plan
- Inhaler technique review

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| SABA (e.g., Salbutamol) | 100-200 mcg PRN | Reliever therapy | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| ICS (e.g., Beclometasone) | Varies (e.g., 200-800 mcg/day) | Preventer therapy | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia |
| LABA (e.g., Salmeterol) | 50 mcg BD | Add-on preventer (always with ICS) | Tremor, palpitations |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) + Short-Acting Beta-Agonist (SABA) PRN, or low-dose ICS-Formoterol PRN.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening exacerbation)
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure
- Airway remodeling (chronic irreversible obstruction)

**Most serious complication ::** Acute severe/life-threatening asthma leading to respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient presenting with episodic wheezing, shortness of breath, and a nighttime cough, often with a history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to confirm the obstructive pattern and demonstrate reversibility, distinguishing it from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, securing the airway, administering high-flow oxygen, and providing bronchodilators (Salbutamol) and systemic steroids.
