# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.
> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, and variable respiratory symptoms.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Trigger-induced airway inflammation leads to bronchoconstriction, mucus hypersecretion, and airway wall remodeling over time.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic wheezing
- Breathlessness (dyspnea)
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Widespread polyphonic wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles of respiration (in severe exacerbations)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.70) with reversibility after bronchodilator | Demonstrate reversible airflow limitation |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Monitor variability and severity |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- History of variable respiratory symptoms AND confirmed variable expiratory airflow limitation.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA) via spacer
2. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral prednisolone)
3. Oxygen therapy (if SpO2 < 92%)
4. Ipratropium bromide (in severe cases)
5. Consider IV magnesium sulfate or IV aminophylline in life-threatening cases

### Long-term
- Avoidance of known triggers
- Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) as foundational controller therapy
- Long-acting beta-2 agonists (LABA) as add-on therapy

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200 mcg PRN | Reliever for acute symptoms | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Fluticasone | Variable based on severity | Controller (maintenance) | Oral thrush, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening exacerbation)
- Airway remodeling
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Status asthmaticus (acute severe asthma leading to respiratory failure).

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with episodic wheeze, cough, and shortness of breath, often worse at night or triggered by allergens/exercise.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to confirm reversible airflow limitation, which is the hallmark of asthma.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute exacerbation, prompt administration of bronchodilators and systemic corticosteroids.
