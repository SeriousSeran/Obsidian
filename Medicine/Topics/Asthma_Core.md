# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Core Concept
- A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and variable respiratory symptoms.

## Pathophysiology
- Exposure to triggers leads to IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation and eosinophilic inflammation.
- Results in smooth muscle contraction (bronchospasm), mucosal edema, and increased mucus production.
- Over time, chronic inflammation can lead to airway remodeling.

## Clinical Features
### Symptoms
- Episodic wheeze, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough.
- Symptoms often worse at night or early morning.
- Triggered by exercise, allergens, cold air, viral infections, or stress.

### Signs
- Expiratory polyphonic wheeze.
- Tachypnea, tachycardia.
- In severe acute attacks: use of accessory muscles, silent chest, cyanosis.

## Management
### Acute
- Oxygen (aim SpO2 94-98%).
- Short-acting beta-agonists (SABA) e.g., Salbutamol (inhaled or nebulized).
- Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone).
- Ipratropium bromide (nebulized) in severe/life-threatening cases.
- Consider IV Magnesium Sulfate in severe acute asthma.

### Long-term
- Stepwise approach:
  1. Low dose Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) + Formoterol (MART - Maintenance and Reliever Therapy) as needed OR SABA as needed + regular low dose ICS.
  2. Increase ICS dose or add Long-Acting Beta Agonist (LABA).
  3. Leukotriene Receptor Antagonists (LTRA) e.g., Montelukast.
  4. Refer to specialist for biologics (e.g., Omalizumab) if severe.
