# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airway obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Exposure to triggers causes airway inflammation (eosinophils, mast cells), leading to smooth muscle constriction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production. Over time, airway remodeling can occur.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheezing
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles in severe attacks
- Tachycardia

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 70%) | Confirms airflow obstruction |
| Reversibility testing | >12% and >200mL increase in FEV1 | Demonstrates reversibility with bronchodilator |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Variable | Used for monitoring and acute severity assessment |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms consistent with asthma.
- Evidence of variable expiratory airflow limitation.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen to maintain SpO2 >94%
2. Salbutamol (SABA) nebulized or via spacer
3. Ipratropium bromide (SAMA) if severe
4. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone or Hydrocortisone)

### Long-term
- Trigger avoidance
- Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Long-acting Beta Agonists (LABA) - usually combined with ICS
- Leukotriene Receptor Antagonists (LTRA)
- Biologics for severe disease (e.g., Omalizumab)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200 mcg PRN | Acute symptom relief | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Fluticasone (ICS) | Varies by severity | Maintenance therapy | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Low-dose Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) + PRN short-acting beta-agonist (SABA), or maintenance and reliever therapy (MART).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Acute severe or life-threatening asthma (status asthmaticus)
- Airway remodeling
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Life-threatening asthma attack leading to respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze, cough at night, and shortness of breath triggered by cold air or exercise.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry with reversibility confirms the obstructive pattern and the characteristic reversibility of asthma, distinguishing it from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute settings, immediate bronchodilation and reducing inflammation with systemic steroids. In chronic, optimizing ICS to prevent exacerbations.

## OSCE Tips

- Ensure to ask about triggers and diurnal variation in history.
- Check inhaler technique.
- Assess severity of acute attacks (e.g., inability to complete sentences, PEF < 50% best/predicted).
