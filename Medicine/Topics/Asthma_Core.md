# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Environmental triggers cause IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation, leading to smooth muscle contraction, mucosal edema, and mucus hypersecretion.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night)

### Signs
- Polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Tachypnoea
- In severe cases: silent chest, cyanosis, exhaustion.

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Reduced | Assesses degree of airflow limitation |
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 70%, reversible | Confirms reversible obstructive pattern |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry showing reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical history and objective demonstration of reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility test.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented reversibility (e.g., FEV1 increase > 12% and 200ml after bronchodilator).

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (target SpO2 94-98%).
2. High-dose inhaled/nebulised bronchodilators (Salbutamol, Ipratropium).
3. Systemic corticosteroids (oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone).

### Long-term
- Avoid triggers, inhaled corticosteroids (ICS), long-acting beta agonists (LABA).

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | Variable (inhaler/nebs) | Acute relief of bronchospasm | Tachycardia, tremor, hypokalemia |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) combined with a short-acting beta agonist (SABA) as needed.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Acute severe asthma exacerbation (status asthmaticus)
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Fatal status asthmaticus.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient presenting with episodic wheeze and night-time cough, often triggered by exercise or cold air.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to objectively prove reversible airflow obstruction, distinguishing asthma from fixed obstruction in COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute severe asthma, restoring oxygenation and reversing bronchospasm immediately with oxygen and high-dose bronchodilators is the priority.

- In a respiratory exam, listen specifically for a polyphonic expiratory wheeze.
- In history taking, always ask about triggers, night symptoms, and previous ICU admissions.
