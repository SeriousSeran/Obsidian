# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** Asthma is a chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by bronchial hyperresponsiveness and reversible airflow obstruction.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Exposure to triggers causes airway inflammation, leading to smooth muscle constriction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production, resulting in airway narrowing.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Use of accessory muscles (in severe cases)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Decreased | Measures degree of airflow obstruction |
| Spirometry | Reversible obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 70%, significant reversibility with bronchodilator) | Confirms diagnosis and reversibility |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry demonstrating reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms suggestive of asthma.
- Evidence of variable airflow obstruction.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Short-acting beta-agonists (SABA) e.g., Salbutamol.
2. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone or Hydrocortisone).
3. Oxygen therapy if hypoxic.

### Long-term
- Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) for maintenance.
- Long-acting beta-agonists (LABA) in combination with ICS.
- Leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRA) if needed.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200 mcg PRN | Reliever for acute symptoms | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Fluticasone | Variable based on severity | Preventer (maintenance) | Oral thrush, hoarseness |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) and a short-acting beta-agonist (SABA) as a reliever.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening acute exacerbation).
- Respiratory failure.
- Pneumothorax.

**Most serious complication ::** Status asthmaticus leading to respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A patient presenting with episodic wheeze, shortness of breath, and a nocturnal cough, often with a history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to confirm the obstructive defect and demonstrate reversibility, which differentiates it from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute exacerbation, restoring adequate oxygenation and relieving airway obstruction rapidly using oxygen, nebulized bronchodilators, and steroids.
