# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.
> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Environmental triggers cause an immune response (often IgE-mediated), leading to airway inflammation, mucosal edema, increased mucus production, and smooth muscle contraction.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze (often expiratory)
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- Widespread polyphonic wheeze
- In severe attacks: use of accessory muscles, paradoxical pulse, silent chest

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) | Reduced | Assesses degree of airflow obstruction |
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7), reversible with bronchodilator | Confirms diagnosis |
| Chest X-ray | Hyperinflation (if severe), or normal | Rule out other causes (e.g., pneumothorax, pneumonia) |
| ABG | Hypoxia, hypocapnia (early), hypercapnia (late/severe) | Assesses severity in acute attacks |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented reversible airflow limitation
- Typical symptoms

---

## Management 💊

### Acute (O SHIT)
1. **O**xygen (target SpO2 94-98%)
2. **S**albutamol (nebulized or inhaled)
3. **H**ydrocortisone (IV) or Prednisolone (oral)
4. **I**pratropium bromide (nebulized)
5. **T**heophylline/Aminophylline (if severe/life-threatening)
6. Magnesium sulfate (IV, for severe/life-threatening)

### Long-term (Stepwise)
- SABA (Short-acting beta agonist) as needed
- ICS (Inhaled corticosteroid)
- LABA (Long-acting beta agonist)
- LTRA (Leukotriene receptor antagonist)
- Biologics (e.g., omalizumab) for severe allergic asthma

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Beclometasone | Varies | Preventer | Oral candidiasis, hoarse voice |

**First-line treatment ::** SABA PRN + low-dose ICS (or ICS/Formoterol PRN depending on guidelines)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax
- Cor pulmonale (rare)

**Most serious complication ::** Fatal asthma / Respiratory arrest

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze, breathlessness, and cough, worse at night or after exercise/allergen exposure.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** PEFR helps grade the severity of an acute attack (moderate, severe, life-threatening).

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, oxygenation and bronchodilation are the immediate priorities.
