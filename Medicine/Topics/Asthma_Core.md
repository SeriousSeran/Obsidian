# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airflow obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, and variable respiratory symptoms.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Chronic airway inflammation mediated by Th2 cells, eosinophils, and IgE, leading to airway remodeling, smooth muscle hypertrophy, mucous hypersecretion, and bronchoconstriction in response to triggers.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic wheeze
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles (in severe exacerbations)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 0.70; Reversibility (>12% and 200mL increase in FEV1 post-bronchodilator) | Confirm obstructive defect and reversibility |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Supportive evidence of variability |
| FeNO | Elevated (>40 ppb in adults) | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversible airflow obstruction.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms suggestive of asthma.
- Variable airflow obstruction.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute (Exacerbation)
1. Oxygen (if saturations < 94%)
2. Salbutamol (SABA) nebulized/inhaled
3. Ipratropium bromide (SAMA) nebulized (if severe)
4. Systemic corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone)
5. Consider IV Magnesium sulfate or Aminophylline for life-threatening exacerbations.

### Long-term
- Avoidance of triggers (allergens, smoke).
- Stepwise pharmacological approach (e.g., GINA guidelines).

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200 mcg PRN | Reliever | Tremor, tachycardia |
| ICS (e.g., Beclometasone) | Varies by dose | Preventer (Step 1/2) | Oral thrush, hoarseness |
| LABA (e.g., Salmeterol) | Varies by inhaler | Add-on (Step 3) | Tachycardia, cramps |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) + short-acting beta-agonist (SABA) PRN, or low-dose ICS/Formoterol as MART (Maintenance and Reliever Therapy).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening exacerbation)
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure
- Cor pulmonale (rare, in chronic severe disease)

**Most serious complication ::** Status asthmaticus leading to fatal respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient with a history of atopy presenting with episodic, nocturnal wheeze and chest tightness.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to objectively demonstrate reversible airflow obstruction, distinguishing asthma from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute severe asthma, assessing severity (PEF <50%, inability to complete sentences in one breath, etc.) and administering immediate high-dose bronchodilators and systemic steroids.
