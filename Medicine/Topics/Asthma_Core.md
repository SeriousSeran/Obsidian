# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Definition
- A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airway obstruction, airway hyperresponsiveness, and recurrent episodes of wheezing, breathlessness, chest tightness, and coughing.

## Clinical Features
- **Symptoms**: Intermittent dyspnoea, wheeze, cough (often nocturnal or early morning), chest tightness.
- **Triggers**: Allergens, exercise, cold air, viral infections, stress, certain medications (e.g., NSAIDs, beta-blockers).

## Investigations
- **Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR)**: Reversibility (diurnal variation or after bronchodilator use).
- **Spirometry**: Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.70) with reversibility (increase in FEV1 > 12% and 200mL after bronchodilator).
- **Fractional exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO)**: Elevated, indicating eosinophilic airway inflammation.

## Management
- **Acute Exacerbation**: Oxygen (if hypoxic), nebulized short-acting beta-agonists (SABA - Salbutamol), nebulized anticholinergics (Ipratropium), systemic corticosteroids (Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone). Consider IV magnesium sulfate or aminophylline in severe/life-threatening cases.
- **Long-term**:
  - Step 1: Low-dose ICS (Inhaled Corticosteroid) + LABA (Long-Acting Beta-Agonist) as maintenance and reliever therapy (MART) or Low-dose ICS + SABA as needed.
  - Step up therapy as per control (increasing ICS dose, adding LTRA or LAMA, referring to specialist).
