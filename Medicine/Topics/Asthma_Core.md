# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Exposure to triggers causes airway inflammation, leading to smooth muscle contraction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production, which causes airway narrowing.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze (often expiratory)
- Breathlessness (dyspnoea)
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often nocturnal or early morning)

### Signs
- Tachypnoea
- Tachycardia
- Expiratory wheeze on auscultation
- Use of accessory muscles in severe cases

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) | Decreased | Shows reversible airway obstruction |
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC ratio < 70% | Confirms obstructive pattern |
| Fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry showing reversibility with bronchodilators.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- History of variable respiratory symptoms
- Documented variable expiratory airflow limitation

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. High-flow oxygen if hypoxic
2. Short-acting beta-agonists (SABA) via spacer or nebulizer
3. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone)

### Long-term
- Identify and avoid triggers
- Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) as preventer
- Long-acting beta-agonists (LABA) added if needed

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | Varies | Reliever for acute symptoms | Tachycardia, tremor |
| Fluticasone (ICS) | Varies | Preventer | Oral thrush (wash mouth) |

**First-line treatment ::** SABA for symptom relief; ICS for regular prevention.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening acute severe asthma)
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Acute severe/life-threatening asthma leading to respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient presenting with episodic wheeze, breathlessness, and nocturnal cough triggered by cold air or exercise.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing differentiates asthma from fixed airway obstruction like COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute severe asthma, oxygenation and bronchodilation are immediate priorities.
