# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and airway inflammation.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Trigger exposure leads to IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation, releasing histamine and leukotrienes. This causes bronchoconstriction, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production, leading to narrowed airways.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic shortness of breath
- Wheezing
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory wheeze on auscultation
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.70) | Confirms airway obstruction. |
| Reversibility testing | >12% and 200mL increase in FEV1 post-bronchodilator | Demonstrates reversible nature of the disease. |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Variable; decreased during exacerbations | Used for monitoring severity and variability. |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Demonstration of variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- History of variable respiratory symptoms
- Evidence of variable expiratory airflow limitation

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. SABA (e.g., Albuterol) via nebulizer or MDI
2. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone)
3. Ipratropium bromide (for moderate/severe exacerbations)

### Long-term
- SABA as needed (reliever)
- Low-dose Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Long-Acting Beta-Agonists (LABA) added if not controlled on ICS

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Albuterol (Salbutamol) | 100-200 mcg PRN | Acute relief of bronchospasm | Tachycardia, tremor, hypokalemia |
| Fluticasone (ICS) | Varies by severity | Maintenance therapy | Oral thrush, hoarseness |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) + SABA PRN

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening severe exacerbation)
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Status asthmaticus

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient presenting with recurrent episodes of wheezing, shortness of breath, and night-time coughing, often triggered by allergens, exercise, or cold air.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to confirm the obstructive pattern and demonstrate reversibility, distinguishing asthma from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute severe attack, securing the airway, providing high-flow oxygen, and administering bronchodilators and systemic steroids immediately.
