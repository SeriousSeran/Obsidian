# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Exposure to triggers causes IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation, leading to bronchospasm, mucosal edema, and increased mucus production, which narrows the airways.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Expiratory polyphonic wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles of respiration (in severe cases)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Reduced | Assesses degree of airflow obstruction. |
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 0.70 with reversibility | Confirms obstructive pattern and reversibility with bronchodilators. |
| Fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation. |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical history combined with objective demonstration of variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented wheeze, breathlessness, chest tightness, or cough.
- Reversibility: improvement in FEV1 by >= 12% and >= 200mL after bronchodilator.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (titrate to saturations 94-98%).
2. Salbutamol (SABA) nebulizers / inhaler via spacer.
3. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone).
4. Ipratropium bromide (SAMA) nebulizers (if severe).

### Long-term
- Avoid triggers (e.g., allergens, smoking).
- Stepwise pharmacological approach (SABA PRN -> low-dose ICS -> add LABA, etc.).

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200 mcg PRN | Reliever therapy for acute symptoms | Tachycardia, tremor, hypokalemia |
| Beclometasone (ICS) | Varies | Preventer therapy for chronic management | Oral candidiasis, hoarse voice |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled short-acting beta2-agonist (SABA) as required, plus an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS) for maintenance.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Acute severe / life-threatening asthma exacerbation
- Pneumothorax
- Chronic airway remodeling

**Most serious complication ::** Life-threatening acute asthma attack leading to respiratory failure or cardiac arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient presenting with episodic wheeze, breathlessness, and nocturnal cough triggered by cold air or exercise.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to objectively confirm reversible airway obstruction, distinguishing it from fixed obstruction seen in COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute exacerbation, ensuring adequate oxygenation and rapidly reversing bronchospasm with a SABA and administering systemic corticosteroids.
