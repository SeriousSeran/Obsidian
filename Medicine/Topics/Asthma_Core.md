# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Environmental triggers cause an immune response (often IgE-mediated), leading to airway inflammation, smooth muscle contraction, and mucus hypersecretion.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often nocturnal or early morning)

### Signs
- Polyphonic wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles in severe attacks
- Pulsus paradoxus (in severe cases)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Reduced | Assesses degree of airflow obstruction |
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 0.7 | Demonstrates obstructive pattern, usually reversible with bronchodilators |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry demonstrating reversible airway obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented reversible airflow obstruction (FEV1 increases by >12% and 200mL after bronchodilator).

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. High-flow oxygen (if hypoxemic)
2. Salbutamol nebulizers
3. Systemic corticosteroids (e.g., oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone)

### Long-term
- Identify and avoid triggers
- Stepwise pharmacological approach (SABA, ICS, LABA)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever for acute symptoms | Tachycardia, tremor |
| Beclometasone (ICS) | Varies | Preventer for chronic inflammation | Oral thrush, hoarseness |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) along with Short-Acting Beta Agonists (SABA) as needed.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Respiratory arrest / fatal asthma attack

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient presenting with episodic wheeze, shortness of breath, and nocturnal cough, often with a personal or family history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to objectively confirm airway obstruction and demonstrate its reversibility, which is the hallmark of asthma.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, the priority is to correct hypoxia with oxygen and reverse bronchospasm with high-dose nebulized bronchodilators.

**What is Asthma? ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease causing reversible obstruction.

**Classic triad/presentation of Asthma ::** Wheeze, breathlessness, and cough (often worse at night).
