# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by bronchial hyperresponsiveness, mucosal edema, and airflow obstruction that is typically reversible.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Triggers (allergens, viral infections, cold air, exercise) cause an immune response (often Th2 mediated with IgE, eosinophils, mast cells). This leads to acute bronchoconstriction, airway edema, and mucus plug formation, obstructing airflow (especially expiratory). Chronic inflammation can lead to airway remodeling.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Episodic wheezing
- Breathlessness / dyspnea
- Chest tightness
- Cough (especially nocturnal or early morning)

### Signs
- Polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Use of accessory muscles (in severe exacerbations)
- Pulsus paradoxus (in severe asthma)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 70%, with reversibility (>12% and >200mL increase in FEV1) post-bronchodilator | Confirms reversible airway obstruction |
| Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) | Variable, often lower in morning | Assesses variability and severity of obstruction |
| Fraction of Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Spirometry showing reversible airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented reversible airflow obstruction.
- FeNO $\ge$ 40 ppb.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute Exacerbation
1.  **Oxygen:** Titrate to SpO2 94-98%.
2.  **Bronchodilators:** Salbutamol nebulized or via spacer (ipratropium if severe).
3.  **Steroids:** Oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone.
4.  **Consider:** IV Magnesium sulfate if not responding.

### Long-term (Stepwise approach)
- SABA (short-acting beta-agonist) PRN (now often combined with ICS as MART).
- ICS (inhaled corticosteroid) low dose.
- Add LABA (long-acting beta-agonist) to ICS.
- Increase ICS dose, add LTRA (leukotriene receptor antagonist).
- Refer to specialist for biologics (e.g., Omalizumab, Mepolizumab) or oral steroids.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever / acute symptom relief | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| Beclometasone | Varies (e.g. 200-800mcg/day) | Preventer / baseline controller | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) combined with a reliever (SABA or formoterol-ICS).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus (life-threatening exacerbation)
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure (Type II if exhausted)
- Airway remodeling (chronic irreversible obstruction)

**Most serious complication ::** Status asthmaticus leading to respiratory arrest

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient presenting with episodic wheeze and nocturnal cough, worsened by cold air or exercise.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used over PEFR for diagnosis because it provides a more reliable and standardized measure of FEV1/FVC ratio and reversibility.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute severe attack, immediate administration of high-flow oxygen, nebulized salbutamol, and systemic steroids.

## OSCE Tips

- In a respiratory exam, look specifically for inhalers by the bedside.
- When auscultating, explicitly listen for polyphonic wheeze and note if it is inspiratory or expiratory (asthma is typically expiratory).
- In a history station, always ask about nocturnal symptoms, triggers, and previous ICU admissions (marker of severity).
