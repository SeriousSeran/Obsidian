# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways characterized by reversible airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and airway inflammation.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Triggers (allergens, viral infections, exercise) cause an inflammatory response mediated by Th2 cells, IgE, mast cells, eosinophils, and other inflammatory cells. This leads to airway edema, mucous plugging, and smooth muscle bronchoconstriction.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze (often worse at night or early morning)
- Breathlessness
- Chest tightness
- Cough

### Signs
- Widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Hyperinflated chest
- In severe attacks: use of accessory muscles, paradoxical pulse, silent chest (life-threatening)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7), reversible with bronchodilator | Demonstrates reversible airway obstruction |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Shows variability in airflow limitation |
| FeNO (Fractional exhaled nitric oxide) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry showing reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical diagnosis supported by objective tests demonstrating variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry with bronchodilator reversibility testing.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Documented reversible airflow limitation (e.g., FEV1 increases by >12% and 200mL after bronchodilator).

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity (Mild/Mod, Severe, Life-threatening).
2. Oxygen (titrate to SpO2 94-98%).
3. Salbutamol nebulized.
4. Ipratropium bromide nebulized (if severe).
5. Systemic corticosteroids (oral prednisolone or IV hydrocortisone).

### Long-term
- Avoid triggers.
- Inhaled corticosteroids (ICS) are the mainstay of preventer therapy.
- Stepwise approach adding LABA, LTRA, etc., based on symptom control.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever (SABA) | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Fluticasone (ICS) | Varies by severity | Preventer | Oral candidiasis, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) plus SABA PRN, or ICS-Formoterol as MART.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Status asthmaticus
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax
- Medication side effects

**Most serious complication ::** Life-threatening acute severe asthma leading to respiratory arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with episodic wheeze, cough, and shortness of breath, often worse at night, with a personal or family history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to prove the obstruction is *reversible*, differentiating it from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute severe attack, immediate nebulized bronchodilators, systemic steroids, and oxygen.
