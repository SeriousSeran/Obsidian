# Asthma

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction and bronchospasm.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Trigger exposure leads to IgE-mediated mast cell degranulation, releasing histamine and leukotrienes, causing bronchoconstriction, airway edema, and mucus hypersecretion.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheeze
- Cough (often nocturnal)
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness

### Signs
- Tachypnea
- Expiratory polyphonic wheeze
- Hyperinflated chest
- Use of accessory muscles (in severe cases)

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | Obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 70%), reversible with bronchodilator | Confirms diagnosis and reversibility |
| Peak Expiratory Flow (PEF) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Monitors control and severity |
| Fractional exhaled nitric oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical history combined with objective demonstration of variable airflow obstruction.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversibility (FEV1 increase >12% and >200mL).

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- British Thoracic Society (BTS) / SIGN guidelines for diagnosis and management.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (titrate to 94-98%)
2. Salbutamol nebulized
3. Ipratropium bromide nebulized
4. Hydrocortisone IV or Prednisolone PO

### Long-term
- Avoidance of triggers
- Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Long-Acting Beta Agonists (LABA)
- Leukotriene Receptor Antagonists (LTRA)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever | Tremor, tachycardia |
| Beclometasone (ICS) | Varies | Preventer | Oral thrush, dysphonia |

**First-line treatment ::** Low-dose Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Life-threatening asthma attack
- Pneumothorax
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Life-threatening or fatal asthma attack

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze and nocturnal cough, often with a personal or family history of atopy.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to objectively confirm the obstructive pattern and demonstrate reversibility, which is the hallmark of asthma.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In an acute attack, the priority is to correct hypoxemia and reverse airflow obstruction with bronchodilators and systemic steroids.

## OSCE Tips

- In a respiratory exam, listen specifically for expiratory wheeze and note the patient's work of breathing.
- Take a thorough history regarding triggers (e.g., pets, dust, cold air, exercise) and nocturnal awakenings.
