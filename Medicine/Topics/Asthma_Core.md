# Asthma Core

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory disease of the airways characterized by reversible airflow obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness, and variable symptoms.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Triggers lead to airway inflammation involving eosinophils, mast cells, and T-helper 2 cells, causing bronchospasm, mucus hypersecretion, and airway remodeling over time.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Wheezing, often expiratory
- Breathlessness (dyspnea)
- Chest tightness
- Cough, often worse at night or early morning

### Signs
- Widespread polyphonic wheeze on auscultation
- Tachypnea
- Use of accessory muscles in severe cases
- Tachycardia

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Peak Expiratory Flow Rate (PEFR) | Reduced variability, improves with bronchodilators | Assess severity and response |
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC < 70%, reversible (>12% or 200ml) with SABA | Objective demonstration of reversible obstruction |
| Fractional Exhaled Nitric Oxide (FeNO) | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry with reversibility testing

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical presentation combined with objective evidence of variable airflow obstruction (Spirometry).

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms + Reversible obstruction on spirometry or PEFR variability.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen (if hypoxic)
2. Inhaled Short-Acting Beta-Agonists (SABA) (e.g., Salbutamol) via spacer or nebulizer
3. Oral or intravenous corticosteroids (e.g., Prednisolone/Hydrocortisone)

### Long-term
- Avoid triggers
- Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)
- Long-Acting Beta-Agonists (LABA) (usually in combination with ICS)
- Leukotriene receptor antagonists (LTRA) (e.g., Montelukast)

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever, acute symptom control | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| Beclomethasone | Varies (e.g., 200-400mcg BID) | Preventer, background inflammation | Oral thrush, hoarseness |

**First-line treatment ::** Inhaled Corticosteroid (ICS) combined with a SABA as needed (or MART - Maintenance and Reliever Therapy using ICS/Formoterol).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Life-threatening asthma (silent chest, exhaustion, cyanosis, poor respiratory effort)
- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax

**Most serious complication ::** Fatal asthma attack leading to hypoxic cardiac arrest.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A young patient presenting with intermittent wheeze, particularly at night, or triggered by exercise, cold air, or allergens.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry to objectively confirm reversible airway obstruction.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute severe asthma, oxygenation and bronchodilation (Salbutamol nebulizers) followed by systemic corticosteroids.
