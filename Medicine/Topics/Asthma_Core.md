# Asthma Core

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** A chronic inflammatory airway disease characterized by reversible airway obstruction and bronchial hyperresponsiveness.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Exposure to a trigger causes an immune response (IgE-mediated), leading to mast cell degranulation, histamine release, airway smooth muscle constriction, mucous hypersecretion, and mucosal edema.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Intermittent dyspnea (shortness of breath)
- Wheezing
- Chest tightness
- Cough (often worse at night or early morning)

### Signs
- Polyphonic expiratory wheeze
- Tachypnea
- Tachycardia
- Prolonged expiratory phase
- In severe attacks: use of accessory muscles, silent chest, cyanosis

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spirometry | FEV1/FVC ratio < 70%, reversible with bronchodilator | Demonstrates reversible airway obstruction |
| PEFR (Peak Flow) | Diurnal variation > 20% | Shows variability in airway caliber over days |
| FeNO | Elevated | Indicates eosinophilic airway inflammation |

**Key investigation ::** Spirometry demonstrating reversibility.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard investigation ::** Spirometry showing obstructive pattern (FEV1/FVC < 0.7) that is reversible (FEV1 improves by >12% and >200mL after bronchodilator).

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- Symptoms matching pattern
- Variable PEFR
- Positive reversibility testing

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. **Oxygen:** If hypoxemic (target SpO2 94-98%).
2. **Bronchodilators:** Nebulized or high-dose inhaled Salbutamol (SABA) +/- Ipratropium (SAMA).
3. **Steroids:** Oral Prednisolone or IV Hydrocortisone to reduce inflammation.

### Long-term
- Avoid triggers.
- Stepwise pharmacological management.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Salbutamol (SABA) | 100-200mcg PRN | Reliever for acute symptoms | Tremor, tachycardia, hypokalemia |
| Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS) | Varies | Preventer (reduces inflammation) | Oral thrush, hoarse voice |

**First-line treatment ::** SABA as required, often combined early with low-dose ICS.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Respiratory failure
- Pneumothorax
- Status asthmaticus (severe acute asthma unresponsive to initial treatment)

**Most serious complication ::** Fatal life-threatening asthma (respiratory arrest).

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Young patient with intermittent wheeze and night-time cough, triggered by exercise or cold air.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Spirometry is used to objectively prove reversible obstruction, differentiating it from COPD.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** In acute severe asthma, the priority is bronchodilation and systemic steroids.

## OSCE Tips

- In a respiratory exam, listen specifically for the widespread polyphonic expiratory wheeze.
- Check inhaler technique if the station is a counseling station.
- Look for signs of severe asthma: inability to complete sentences in one breath, tachycardia, tachypnea.
