# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital setting, leading to consolidation and impaired gas exchange.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Inhalation or aspiration of pathogens (most commonly Streptococcus pneumoniae) overwhelms alveolar macrophages, leading to an inflammatory response, exudate formation, and alveolar filling (consolidation).

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Fever, rigors
- Productive cough (purulent or rust-colored sputum)
- Dyspnea
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Systemic symptoms (fatigue, myalgia)

### Signs
- Tachypnea, tachycardia
- Focal bronchial breathing
- Dullness to percussion
- Coarse crackles / crepitations
- Increased vocal resonance / tactile fremitus

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray (PA & Lateral) | Lobar or patchy consolidation | Confirms diagnosis and extent |
| FBC / CRP | Leukocytosis (neutrophilia), elevated CRP | Markers of acute bacterial infection |
| U&Es, LFTs | Possible AKI or derangements | Helps calculate CURB-65 score, assess organ dysfunction |
| Blood cultures / Sputum culture | May isolate causative organism | Guides targeted antibiotic therapy, especially in severe cases |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray (PA)

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical signs of pneumonia combined with new infiltrates/consolidation on Chest X-ray.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray (PA)

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 Score (Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30/min, Blood pressure <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic, Age ≥65).
- CRB-65 (for primary care).

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity using CURB-65 (0-1: Outpatient, 2: Hospitalize, 3+: Severe, consider ICU).
2. Oxygen therapy to maintain SpO2 >94% (88-92% if at risk of type II respiratory failure).
3. Fluid resuscitation if hypotensive or dehydrated.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations.
- Follow-up chest X-ray at 6 weeks to ensure resolution (exclude underlying malignancy).

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg TDS PO (typical oral) | First-line for low severity CAP | GI upset, rash, anaphylaxis (penicillin allergy) |
| Doxycycline / Macrolide | Variable | Alternative if penicillin-allergic | GI upset, photosensitivity (doxy), QT prolongation (macrolide) |
| Co-amoxiclav + Clarithromycin | Variable (often IV) | High severity CAP | GI upset, cholestasis (co-amoxiclav) |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (for typical mild-to-moderate CAP)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion / Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis / Septic shock
- Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis / Septic shock

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** A patient presenting with an acute onset of fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and focal crepitations on auscultation.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Chest X-ray is required to confirm the presence of consolidation and rule out mimics like pleural effusion or heart failure.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Immediate administration of empirical antibiotics according to local guidelines, based on severity assessment (CURB-65), and ensuring adequate oxygenation.

## OSCE Tips

- Always check respiratory rate and oxygen saturations first.
- Ask about recent travel, occupation, and sick contacts to identify atypical organisms (e.g., Legionella).
- Explain the CURB-65 score clearly to the examiner to justify your management plan (home vs. admission).
