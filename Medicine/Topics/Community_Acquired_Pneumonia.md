# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital setting, typically presenting with respiratory symptoms and new infiltrates on chest imaging.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Microorganisms (most commonly *Streptococcus pneumoniae*) bypass upper airway defenses or are microaspirated into the alveoli, leading to an inflammatory response, alveolar exudate, and impaired gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Fever, chills, rigors
- Cough (often productive of purulent sputum)
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Dyspnea

### Signs
- Tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxia
- Dullness to percussion
- Bronchial breath sounds
- Coarse crackles

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Lobar consolidation or patchy infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism (e.g., Gram-positive diplococci) | Guide targeted antibiotic therapy |
| Blood cultures | Bacteremia (in severe cases) | Detect systemic spread |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray (CXR) showing new consolidation.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical presentation consistent with pneumonia AND new pulmonary infiltrate on chest imaging.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 score (Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate >=30/min, Blood pressure <90/60 mmHg, Age >=65) to assess severity and guide management site.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess ABCs and oxygenate if hypoxic (target SpO2 >94% or 88-92% in risk of type II respiratory failure).
2. Assess severity using CURB-65 score.
3. Administer empirical antibiotics promptly.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | 500mg TDS PO | Mild CAP (CURB-65 0-1) | GI upset, rash, allergy |
| Amoxicillin + Macrolide | Varies | Moderate CAP (CURB-65 2) | QT prolongation (macrolide) |
| Co-amoxiclav + Macrolide | Varies | Severe CAP (CURB-65 3-5) | Hepatotoxicity, C. diff |

**First-line treatment ::** Oral amoxicillin for mild, uncomplicated CAP.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis / Septic shock

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis with multi-organ failure.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Patient with sudden onset high fever, productive cough with "rusty" sputum, and pleuritic chest pain.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Sputum culture helps identify the specific organism to narrow antibiotic therapy, reducing resistance risk.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Early administration of appropriate empirical antibiotics based on local guidelines and severity score.
