# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending
> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** Community Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital setting, presenting with symptoms of acute infection and accompanied by the presence of an acute infiltrate on chest radiograph.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Infection occurs when virulent microorganisms reach the lower respiratory tract and overwhelm host defence mechanisms (e.g., cough reflex, mucociliary clearance, alveolar macrophages). This leads to an inflammatory response, alveolar exudate, and consolidation, impairing gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Cough (often productive)
- Fever, chills, rigors
- Dyspnoea
- Pleuritic chest pain
- Sputum production (purulent or rust-coloured)
- Systemic symptoms: fatigue, myalgia

### Signs
- Tachypnoea
- Tachycardia
- Fever
- Signs of consolidation: dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, increased vocal resonance, crackles/crepitations
- Hypoxia

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-Ray (CXR) | Lobar consolidation, patchy infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis, assess extent |
| FBC/CRP | Elevated WCC (neutrophilia), elevated CRP | Markers of systemic inflammation/infection |
| Sputum Culture | Causative organism (e.g., S. pneumoniae) | Guide targeted antibiotic therapy |
| Blood Cultures | May be positive in severe cases | Identify bacteremia |
| U&Es | Abnormal urea | CURB-65 scoring, hydration status |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-Ray (CXR) is the gold standard for confirmation.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical presentation of acute infection + new infiltrate on chest radiograph.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-Ray (CXR)

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- **CURB-65 Score:** Predicts 30-day mortality and guides site of care.
  - **C**onfusion (AMTS <= 8)
  - **U**rea > 7 mmol/L
  - **R**espiratory rate >= 30 breaths/min
  - **B**lood pressure (SBP < 90 mmHg or DBP <= 60 mmHg)
  - Age >= **65** years

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. **Oxygen Therapy:** Titrate to maintain saturations 94-98% (or 88-92% if risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure).
2. **Empirical Antibiotics:** Based on local guidelines and CURB-65 severity.
3. **Fluid Resuscitation:** IV fluids if hypotensive or dehydrated.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice
- Pneumococcal and Influenza vaccinations for at-risk groups
- Follow-up CXR at 6 weeks to ensure resolution and exclude underlying malignancy

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | e.g. 500mg-1g TDS (varies by severity/guideline) | Mild to moderate CAP (typical coverage) | GI upset, rash, anaphylaxis (penicillin allergy) |
| Macrolide (e.g., Clarithromycin) | e.g. 500mg BD | Atypical coverage or penicillin allergy | GI upset, QT prolongation |
| Co-amoxiclav + Macrolide | IV/PO (varies) | Severe CAP | GI upset, rash, hepatotoxicity |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (oral) is often first-line for low-severity CAP, with a macrolide added for atypical coverage if suspected or severity increases. (Always follow local guidelines).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis / Septic shock
- Respiratory failure (Type 1)

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis with multi-organ failure.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Typical organism: Streptococcus pneumoniae (rust-coloured sputum, sudden onset fever, rigors, lobar consolidation). Atypical organism: Mycoplasma pneumoniae (younger patient, gradual onset, extrapulmonary symptoms like erythema multiforme, haemolytic anaemia).

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Urea in CURB-65 reflects dehydration and renal hypoperfusion secondary to systemic illness/sepsis.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Early administration of appropriate empirical antibiotics and oxygen therapy.

## 📇 Flashcards

**What is the most common bacterial cause of Community Acquired Pneumonia? ::** Streptococcus pneumoniae.

**What are the components of the CURB-65 score? ::** Confusion, Urea > 7, RR >= 30, BP (SBP<90 or DBP<=60), Age >= 65.

**What finding on percussion is classically associated with pneumonia (consolidation)? ::** Dullness to percussion.

**What are the key auscultatory findings in lobar pneumonia? ::** Bronchial breathing, coarse crackles/crepitations, increased vocal resonance.

**When should a follow-up chest X-ray be performed after an episode of pneumonia? ::** At 6 weeks (to ensure resolution and rule out underlying malignancy, especially in smokers/older adults).

## OSCE Tips

- In a respiratory exam, explicitly mention looking for sputum pots during the general inspection.
- Ensure you perform vocal resonance or tactile vocal fremitus properly if you suspect consolidation.
- If you hear bronchial breathing, check for whispering pectoriloquy.
