# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Draft | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside of a healthcare setting.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Inhalation or aspiration of pathogens into the lower respiratory tract, leading to alveolar inflammation, fluid exudation, and consolidation, impairing gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Cough (productive or dry)
- Fever, rigors
- Dyspnea
- Pleuritic chest pain

### Signs
- Tachypnea, tachycardia
- Hypoxia
- Bronchial breath sounds
- Dullness to percussion
- Coarse crackles

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray | Consolidation/infiltrate | Confirm diagnosis, rule out effusion |
| FBC/CRP | Elevated WCC, elevated CRP | Assess inflammatory response |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism | Guide targeted antibiotic therapy |
| Blood cultures | Bacteremia | Identify systemic infection |
| ABG | Hypoxia, respiratory failure | Assess severity in severe cases |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray showing new infiltrate/consolidation.

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical presentation combined with a new infiltrate on Chest X-ray.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray.

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 (Confusion, Urea >7, Respiratory rate >=30, Blood pressure <90/60, Age >=65) to assess severity and mortality risk.

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Assess severity using CURB-65.
2. Oxygen therapy to maintain sats >94% (or 88-92% in risk of type 2 resp failure).
3. Fluid resuscitation if hypotensive.
4. Prompt empirical antibiotics (within 4 hours).

### Long-term
- Follow-up chest X-ray at 6 weeks to ensure resolution and rule out underlying malignancy.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | e.g. 500mg TDS | Low severity CAP (empirical) | GI upset, allergy |
| Macrolide (e.g. Clarithromycin) | | Atypical coverage or penicillin allergy | QT prolongation, GI upset |
| Co-amoxiclav + Macrolide | | Moderate/High severity CAP | GI upset, allergy |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (in mild CAP) or as per local guidelines.

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Pleural effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis
- Respiratory failure

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis / septic shock.

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Typical symptoms (fever, cough, purulent sputum, pleuritic pain) with focal chest signs and CXR consolidation.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Sputum and blood cultures are crucial in severe CAP to narrow down antibiotic therapy, especially with rising resistance.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Early administration of empirical antibiotics and adequate oxygenation.
