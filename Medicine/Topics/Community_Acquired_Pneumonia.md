# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> Not final clinical advice. Learning material only.

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital setting.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Pathogens bypass respiratory defense mechanisms or overwhelm the local immune response, leading to inflammation, fluid and pus filling the alveoli (consolidation), impairing gas exchange.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Fever, chills
- Productive cough (purulent or rust-colored sputum)
- Dyspnea
- Pleuritic chest pain

### Signs
- Tachypnea, tachycardia, hypoxia
- Dullness to percussion
- Bronchial breath sounds
- Coarse crackles
- Increased tactile vocal fremitus/vocal resonance

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-Ray | Lobar consolidation or patchy infiltrates | Confirm diagnosis, rule out complications |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism | Target antibiotic therapy |
| Blood cultures | Bacteremia (in severe cases) | Guide targeted therapy |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-Ray

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical signs of pneumonia with new infiltrates on chest radiograph.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-Ray

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- CURB-65 score (Confusion, Urea >7 mmol/L, Respiratory rate ≥30, Blood pressure <90 systolic or ≤60 diastolic, Age ≥65)

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. Oxygen therapy if hypoxic.
2. Fluid resuscitation if hypotensive.
3. Empiric antibiotics based on severity and local guidelines.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccination.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | Varies | Mild CAP | GI upset, rash |
| Macrolide or Doxycycline | Varies | Atypical coverage or penicillin allergy | GI upset, QTc prolongation (macrolides) |

**First-line treatment ::** Amoxicillin (for mild CAP, adjust per guidelines)

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Parapneumonic effusion
- Empyema
- Lung abscess
- Sepsis

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Acute onset of fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and signs of consolidation on exam.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** Chest X-ray to confirm the presence of consolidation and rule out other causes of symptoms.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Early administration of appropriate antibiotics and assessment of severity using CURB-65.

**What is Community Acquired Pneumonia? ::** An acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma acquired outside a hospital.

**Classic triad/presentation of CAP ::** Fever, cough with purulent sputum, and dyspnea/pleuritic chest pain.
