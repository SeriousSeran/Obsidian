# Community Acquired Pneumonia

> **Domain:** Medicine | **Status:** Active | **Review:** Pending

---

## Definition

> What is this condition/concept in one clear sentence?

**Definition ::** An acute infection of the lung parenchyma acquired outside of a hospital setting, typically presenting with cough, fever, and radiological evidence of consolidation.

---

## Pathophysiology 🔬

> Mechanism — what goes wrong at the cellular or physiological level?

**Pathophysiology ::** Microorganisms (usually bacteria, like Streptococcus pneumoniae) overwhelm the respiratory defense mechanisms (cilia, macrophages), leading to an inflammatory response in the alveoli. This causes alveolar exudate (fluid and pus) to accumulate, impairing gas exchange and causing consolidation.

---

## Clinical Features 🩺

### Symptoms
- Productive cough (purulent or rust-colored sputum)
- Fever, rigors
- Pleuritic chest pain (worse on inspiration)
- Dyspnea (shortness of breath)

### Signs
- Tachypnea (increased respiratory rate)
- Tachycardia
- Hypoxia
- Focal signs of consolidation: dullness to percussion, bronchial breathing, coarse crackles, increased vocal resonance.

### Investigations
| Test | Expected Finding | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Chest X-ray (CXR) | Lobar consolidation or patchy infiltrates | To confirm diagnosis and assess severity |
| Blood tests (FBC, CRP, U&E) | Raised WCC (neutrophilia), raised CRP, evaluate urea | To assess inflammatory response and calculate CURB-65 |
| Sputum culture | Causative organism identified | To guide targeted antibiotic therapy |

**Key investigation ::** Chest X-ray (CXR)

---

## Diagnosis

**Gold standard:**
> Clinical presentation compatible with pneumonia combined with new infiltrates on a chest X-ray.

**Gold standard investigation ::** Chest X-ray

**Clinical criteria / scoring:**
- **CURB-65 Score:** Used to assess severity and mortality risk.
  - **C**onfusion (Abbreviated Mental Test Score <= 8)
  - **U**rea > 7 mmol/L
  - **R**espiratory Rate >= 30/min
  - **B**lood Pressure (Systolic < 90 mmHg or Diastolic <= 60 mmHg)
  - Age >= **65** years

---

## Management 💊

### Acute
1. **Oxygen:** If hypoxic (SpO2 < 94% or custom target for COPD).
2. **Antibiotics:** Empirical treatment based on local guidelines (e.g., Amoxicillin or Macrolide for mild; co-amoxiclav + macrolide for severe).
3. **Fluids:** IV fluids if hypotensive or dehydrated.

### Long-term
- Smoking cessation advice.
- Pneumococcal and influenza vaccinations for at-risk groups.
- Follow-up CXR at 6 weeks to ensure resolution and rule out underlying malignancy.

### Medications
| Drug | Dose | Indication | Key Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amoxicillin | Varies (e.g., 500mg TDS oral) | First-line empirical for mild CAP | Penicillin allergy, GI upset |
| Clarithromycin | Varies (e.g., 500mg BD oral) | Atypical coverage or penicillin allergy | GI upset, QT prolongation |

**First-line treatment ::** Appropriate empirical antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin for mild, low-risk cases, as per local protocol).

---

## Complications ⚠️

- Pleural effusion
- Empyema
- Sepsis
- Lung abscess

**Most serious complication ::** Sepsis

---

## Viva Points 🎯

> What examiners love to ask about this topic.

**Viva Q — Classic presentation ::** Elderly patient with fever, productive cough, pleuritic chest pain, and focal crackles.

**Viva Q — Why this investigation? ::** CXR is needed to confirm the diagnosis and rule out other causes like heart failure or malignancy. Urea is checked to calculate the CURB-65 score.

**Viva Q — Management priority ::** Assess severity using CURB-65, provide oxygen if hypoxic, and administer appropriate empirical antibiotics promptly.

**What is the most common bacterial cause of Community Acquired Pneumonia? ::** Streptococcus pneumoniae.

**What are the components of the CURB-65 score? ::** Confusion, Urea >7, Respiratory rate >=30, Blood pressure <90 systolic or <=60 diastolic, Age >=65.

**What finding on auscultation suggests lung consolidation? ::** Bronchial breathing or coarse crackles.

**Management of mild CAP (score 0-1) ::** Usually managed in the community with oral antibiotics (e.g., Amoxicillin).

- Mention calculating a CURB-65 score in your management plan.
